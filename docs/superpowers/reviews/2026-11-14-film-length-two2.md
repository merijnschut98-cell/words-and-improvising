General finding for the batch. These films are not long because of the ceiling. The lesson text is 860 to 1,025 words and the narration is a compressed paraphrase of it (about 75 to 85 percent), beat for beat, in the lesson's own order. There is almost no ELABORATION-DECOR: nearly everything the film says is in the lesson. The caption duplicates the voice in most beats; that is a screen design choice and not counted as padding. Where the film can be called long, it is because two lessons plus two worked examples need about 5 minutes to say. The "saving" would have to come from cutting lesson points, which the rubric forbids. The opposite risk (dropped points) is small and listed per film.

A note on taster length (60 to 90 s): for every film the taster would keep the two core definitions or rules (one per lesson), one worked example in its short form (the numbers and the answer), and the "scherpe vraag" of each lesson. Details are given per film.

Beat class percentages are estimates by word share of the narration (voice). "Lesson-trivia" beats (names, years, attributions) are classed NEW because they are lesson text; I flag them as the first candidates to cut in a taster.
