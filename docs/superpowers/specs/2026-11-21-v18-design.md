Design pass for the points in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-18-v18-merijn-improvements.md`, in its five groups. Nothing here is built yet. Section 4 lists the decisions that are Merijn's; section 5 gives the order. Where a choice needs Merijn, the design describes the recommended option and marks it **(Q n)**.

## 0. Constraints that shape every group

- **Progress hangs on ids.** Lesson ids are `<moduleId>.<slug>`. `moduleOf` takes the module from the id prefix, so a lesson can never move to another module. Recall questions are positional, `<lessonId>#r<n>`, and review boxes are keyed `lang|<lessonId>#r<n>` (`src/core/coursereview.js`). `tests/progresskeep.test.js` pins the kind (`mc`/`num`) and answer position of every recall slot of 24 v9 lessons and of all v13 modules (`tests/fixtures/v13-*-recall-keys.json`). The index holds per lesson the recall count (`meta.questions`), and the validator requires the files to match it.
- **Volume.** 44 modules, 736 lessons, 3,682 questions (2,945 recall plus pre). 2,870 of them are multiple choice and 812 numeric. Projects: 18 modules, 288 lessons, 1,152 recall questions.
- **Standing rules** (CLAUDE.md): test first; a review agent for every content piece; render a film right after its review; CACHE bumped once per push; every new page, VTT and poster goes in `SHELL`; the four levels (addendum 3) apply to every new topic; films are only as long as their content needs.

## Group 1: Projects structure (DMAIC) and the method families

### 1.1 What exists today

- **The road.** The Projects map (`src/content/courses/maps/pm.js`) draws the lifecycle field as a road of seven stations: `pm.approach`, `pm.intake`, `pm.plan`, `pm.formats`, `pm.run`, `pm.land` and `pm.close`. `roadOrder` and `breadthOrder` (`src/core/course.js`) walk that road first. The route lesson `pm.approach.route` teaches the road as "vijf fasen: Intake, Plannen, Uitvoeren, Landen, Afsluiten". The same five stages appear in `openers.pm.js`, `pm/formats.*.js`, the approach intro film (`films/pm.approach.intro/build.mjs` and its VTTs) and in `films/pm.scaled.hybrid`.
- **Lean in Projects is thin, and Measure and Analyse are missing.** There is no Projects module for measuring or analysing a process. That material sits in Engineering: `eng.improve` (Technisch probleemoplossen, level 3: grondoorzaak, feiten en experimenten, PDCA en A3, kaizen en waardestroom) and `eng.spc`. Some term counts in the Dutch Projects files, against Engineering: Ishikawa 0 (11 in Engineering), 8D 0 (37), Pareto 3 (50). SIPOC (16) and "nulmeting" (96) do appear in Projects. In Projects, root-cause work is one topic in `pm.stuck`, and the baseline appears in `pm.close.proof` and `pm.perform`.
- **The method families lesson, `pm.approach.families`** (`src/content/courses/pm/approach.nl.js` line 292), has three problems:
  - **"Only three methods."** The first reading block says "Er zijn vier methodefamilies … en een mengvorm" but then names only three: Voorspellend, Agile and Hybride. Lean and Six Sigma follow in the next block. The worked example also compares only three ways (Voorspellend, Agile, Six Sigma). The lesson names few actual methods: waterfall, PRINCE2, Scrum, Kanban, A3, kaizen and DMAIC are mentioned, but there is no overview and no PDCA, stage-gate, 8D, DMADV or Critical Chain.
  - **"Punctuation missing."** The source text itself is punctuated. The likely places Merijn saw are these. (a) The run-on first block: a definition, then three families separated only by full stops and semicolons, with lesson titles inside the sentence ("de lessen Een werkwijze kiezen en De vijf events van Scrum leggen die uit"). Without quotes or capitals, that sentence reads as if words are missing. (b) The film subtitle "Er zijn vier methodefamilies (method family) en een mengvorm.", which uses the singular in brackets (`pm.approach.intro.nl.vtt` cue 17). (c) The next cue, "Voorspellend, agile, Lean en Six Sigma.", which drops the mix the cue before announced. The rewrite fixes all three. A screenshot from Merijn would confirm which one he meant, but the fix does not depend on it.
  - **"DMAIC is not only Six Sigma."** The block says "Six Sigma: verbeteren met data, in vaste stappen die DMAIC heten". The term DMAIC is defined as "De vijf stappen van Six Sigma". Recall `#r2` asks "Welke familie past bij een terugkerend probleem waarover je veel data hebt?" with the answer "Six Sigma met DMAIC". The film says "DMAIC, de stappen van Six Sigma". `pm.approach.dmaic` frames DMAIC as a route for "een terugkerend probleem met data" only. Merijn's point is that DMAIC is a project structure that holds any improvement project together, and Lean practitioners use it without the statistics.

### 1.2 Design: DMAIC as the Projects spine (Q1, Q2)

**Recommended (Q1 A): the road becomes Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control. Existing modules are regrouped under the phases, and two new modules fill Measure and Analyse.**

| DMAIC phase | What it holds, Lean-oriented | Modules (ids unchanged) |
|---|---|---|
| Before you start | route, choice of method, tools | `pm.approach` (rewritten route: DMAIC as the spine) |
| **Define** | problem statement, voice of the customer, CTQ, SIPOC, project charter, stakeholders, business case | `pm.intake` |
| **Measure** | gemba walk, current-state value stream map, baseline and data collection plan, operational definitions, a light measurement-system check, spotting the eight wastes | **`pm.measure` (new)** |
| **Analyse** | Pareto, fishbone, 5 why, waste and flow analysis on the VSM, constraint, verifying a cause with data, the left half of the A3 | **`pm.analyse` (new)** |
| **Improve** | choosing and piloting the solution (PDCA), kaizen event, planning and running the implementation | `pm.plan`, `pm.formats`, `pm.run` |
| **Control** | standard work, control plan, visual management, handover, proving the result, closing | `pm.land`, `pm.close` |

The other fields (people, portfolio and consulting, IT and agile) stay as they are.

- **No lesson moves module**, because of ids. The new modules link to existing depth where it lives: `pm.stuck.rootcause`, `eng.improve`, `eng.spc` and `pm.perform`. A Projects lesson takes the project leader's view (what to ask for, what to decide, what good looks like). The Engineering lesson keeps the technique.
- **Each new module has four topics × four levels, 16 lessons per language.** Before anything is written, the proposal lists what each level teaches per topic (addendum 3), and Merijn approves it. Seed topics, to be confirmed by a coverage pass:
  - pm.measure: Gemba and the current state; Baseline and data collection; Waste and flow measures (lead time, WIP, first pass yield); Measurement you can trust.
  - pm.analyse: From symptom to cause; Pareto and stratification; Verifying causes with data; The constraint and the flow.
- **The map.** The lifecycle zone stays one field with one road. Above the stations, five phase bands are labelled D, M, A, I and C, using a new optional `phases` list in the map data (`[{ id: 'define', label: { nl, en }, modules: [...] }]`). The road (`routes`) becomes approach, intake, measure, analyse, plan, formats, run, land, close. The places get narrower or the road takes two rows; `checkMap` keeps the 44 px rule. It gains a check that the phases cover the road modules in road order. The module title stays (Intake, Plannen, …), and the module screen shows the phase as a small pill: "Define · Intake".
- **The lessons.** Every Projects road module gets a DMAIC reference where it is natural. Typically that is one sentence in the first Basis reading block of each topic ("In DMAIC is dit de Define-fase: …") and one opener line per level 2. These edits are done inside the module pass (task P-n, section 3), so each lesson file is touched once.
- **Other structures are named, not hidden.** The rewritten `pm.approach.route` and `pm.approach.families` lay DMAIC next to the other structures, in one picture (group 5) and one table. The structures: PDCA (the small loop inside Improve and Control); A3 (the left half is Define to Analyse, the right half Improve and Control); 8D (DMAIC for one customer complaint, with containment); DMADV / DFSS (when you design something new instead of improving it); PRINCE2 stages and stage-gate (the governance layer over the phases: a gate at each phase end); the PMBOK process groups (a body of knowledge, not a route); Agile (sprints inside Improve when the solution is software or needs fast feedback).
- **Delivery projects (Q2 A).** A line move or an ERP rollout is not an improvement of a running process. The recommendation is still one spine, with a light Measure and Analyse: "measure the current state you must not lose" and "analyse the risks and dependencies". The route lesson says plainly where DMAIC fits less well, and that PRINCE2 or stage-gate stages are then the governance layer over the same phases. The alternative (Q2 B) keeps two roads, which is clearer but doubles what the map and the films have to show.
- **The effect on learners.** `breadthOrder` puts the Basis lessons of pm.measure and pm.analyse before any Werkkennis. Someone who has passed every Projects Basis lesson then sees the new Basis lessons as his next step. That is intended.
- **Films.** Field film 1 (`pm.field.lifecycle`) and the approach intro film (`pm.approach.intro`) name the five stages and assert lesson text in their `build.mjs`. Both are rebuilt and re-rendered after the text changes. The two new modules each need a module intro film (v17 rule). Measure and Analyse are visual (VSM, Pareto), so films earn their place there.

## Group 2: Question quality and new question types

### 2.1 What exists today

- **Schema** (`checkQuestion`, `src/content/courses/validate.js` line 19). A question is either multiple choice (exactly 4 distinct options, `answer` in the options) or numeric (`number`, `tolerance > 0`, `unit`). It also has `q`, `why` and an optional `hint` (at most 25 words, and it must not give the answer). Each lesson has 1–2 `pre` and 3–5 `recall` questions. The validator checks that both languages have the same counts, the same numbers and the same answer positions.
- **Judging** (`judge`, `src/core/course.js` line 23): `answer === question.answer`, or a numeric answer within the tolerance (a decimal comma is accepted).
- **UI** (`src/ui/quiz.js`): option buttons, or a numeric input; a hint panel that can strike out one option; a review card; then the misses again. `onePass` (used by the module check) means no hint, no going back and no retry.
- **The quiz** (`lessonQuiz`): 5–8 questions by level, made of the lesson's recall questions, then generated term questions (`#t<n>`), then recall questions from earlier levels of the topic, and finally the guess question again.
- **Scheduling.** Leitner boxes with intervals 1, 3, 7, 21, 60 and 120 days. Box 4 means owned. A correct answer after a hint does not climb a box. Only recall questions (`#r`) are scheduled.

**The patterns Merijn means, measured over all 2,870 multiple-choice questions:**

| Signal | Count | What it means |
|---|---|---|

| The right answer is the longest option | 26% (748) | close to chance (25%): the length tell has been handled. 254 cases are still more than 30% longer than every other option. |
| Answer position | 695 / 800 / 705 / 670 | evenly spread: fine. |

| Engineering stems of 20 words or more | 35% at Basis to 41% at Expert | Engineering is better: more scenarios and numbers. |

### 2.2 Design: the question standard (applies to every question at every level)

Written down as `docs/superpowers/content/question-standard.md` and enforced by a lint (2.4) and by the reviewer's rubric.

1. **Every distractor must be attractive to a half-informed colleague.** Allowed sources: a real misconception (from the topic's trap list, 2.5); a true statement that does not answer this stem; the right answer to a neighbouring concept; the right idea with a wrong number, order or condition. Not allowed: jokes, obviously lazy or rude behaviour, things nobody does (slides, page counts), and absolutes that no professional would say.
2. **No give-away form.** All options share the same grammar and the same length band (no option more than 1.3× the next longest). An absolute word may appear only if the key or at least one other option also carries one.
3. **The stem carries the situation.** At Werkkennis and above, a decision stem names the role, the moment, the constraint and, where it matters, the numbers. A question that asks "the right reaction" says what is at stake. If two options are defensible under some reading, either the stem rules one out, or the item becomes a best-answer item that says so.
4. **The level shows in the question, not only in the lesson** (addendum 3). Basis: recognise and apply a definition to a plain case. Werkkennis: apply it with simple numbers or steps. Gevorderd: choose between options that each have a cost, in a stated situation. Expert: weigh several factors and spot what is missing. At Gevorderd and Expert at most one recall question per lesson may be a definition or recognition question; the rest are situation or decision items.
5. **The `why` explains the near miss**: the key, and why the most attractive wrong option fails here.
6. **Correctness is checked against the lesson text and a source.** A stem may not rest on a claim the lesson does not make.

### 2.3 Design: new question kinds

A question gets an optional `kind`. Without one it is `mc` (four options) or `num` (has `number`), exactly as today, so all existing content stays valid.

```js
// best answer: MC-shaped, two or more defensible options; the stem says "beste" / "best"
{ kind: 'best', q: '… Wat is de beste eerste stap?', options: [a, b, c, d], answer: b,
  close: c,                       // the runner-up, required
  why: 'b, omdat …; c is ook verdedigbaar maar …' }

// select all that apply: 4–6 options, 1 to n−1 correct
{ kind: 'all', q: '… Welke horen in een projectdefinitie? Vink alle juiste aan.', options: [a, b, c, d, e], answers: [a, c, d], why: '…' }

// fill in the word: one gap, written as ___ in the text
{ kind: 'gap', q: 'Vul het ontbrekende woord in.', text: 'De langste keten van afhankelijke taken heet het ___ pad.',
  accept: ['kritieke', 'kritiek'], choices: null /* or 4–6 words: a word bank, for Basis */, why: '…' }

// put in order (puzzle): 3–6 items, stored in the right order, shown shuffled
{ kind: 'order', q: 'Zet de DMAIC-fasen van dit project in volgorde.', items: [s1, s2, s3, s4, s5], why: '…' }

// match pairs (puzzle): 3–5 pairs, the right side shown shuffled
{ kind: 'match', q: 'Koppel elke situatie aan de aanpak die past.', pairs: [[l1, r1], [l2, r2], [l3, r3], [l4, r4]], why: '…' }
```

**Validation** (`checkQuestion`):
- `best`: same as `mc`, plus `close` must be in the options and differ from `answer`, and the stem must contain "beste" (nl) or "best" (en).
- `all`: 4–6 distinct options; `answers` is a non-empty proper subset of the options. Across a module, not every `all` item may have the same number of keys (otherwise "always two" becomes a tell).
- `gap`: `text` contains exactly one `___`; `accept` has 1–4 entries; `choices`, if present, has 4–6 distinct entries including `accept[0]`. The hint check also covers `accept`.
- `order`: 3–6 distinct `items`. `match`: 3–5 pairs, with distinct left sides and distinct right sides.
- **en/nl parity** (in `validateModule`): the same kind per slot; `best` and `mc` have the key at the same position; `all` has the same key indices; `order` the same item count; `match` the same pair count. `gap` accept lists are free per language.
- The terms-defined-once and markup checks apply to the new fields.

**Judging and scoring** (a new pure file, `src/core/questionkinds.js`; `judge` in `course.js` delegates to it):
- `best`, `mc`: equality. `num`: unchanged.
- `all`: set equality. **Scheduling counts all-or-nothing**; the review shows partial credit ("3 van 4 goed, 1 te veel") **(Q10)**.
- `gap`: normalised equality (lower case, trimmed, diacritics folded, punctuation dropped). One typo (Levenshtein ≤ 1) is accepted for words of 6 letters or more, and is then shown as "bijna goed gespeld: kritieke".
- `order`: exact order is correct. The review marks each item placed wrong.
- `match`: every pair right is correct. The review shows the wrong pairs.
- Scheduling is unchanged: correct and no hint climbs a box; correct with a hint holds; wrong goes to box 1.

**Hints** (`src/core/hint.js`, `questionHint`): `best` strikes the weakest option, never `close`. `all` tells how many are correct. `gap` gives the first letter. `order` places the first item. `match` fixes one pair.

**UI** (`src/ui/quiz.js`; each kind its own small renderer, all 44 px targets, keyboard and screen reader friendly):
- `best` shows a label "Kies het beste antwoord"; otherwise it looks like MC.
- `all`: toggle buttons (`aria-pressed`) and a "Controleer" button.
- `gap`: the sentence with an inline input, Enter to submit; with `choices`, word chips instead of typing.
- `order`: tap-to-place. Tap the items in order; they fill numbered slots; tap a slot to take an item back. No drag needed on a phone, and screen readers can follow it.
- `match`: tap a left item, then its right partner; a pair gets a shared colour and number; tap a pair to undo.
- The review card shows the right set, order or pairs.

**Where the new kinds live (keeping progress safe):**
- **Recall slots keep their kind.** A rewritten `mc` slot may become `best`, because `best` is MC-shaped: the progress-keep test treats `best` as `mc` and checks that the answer position is pinned. A rewrite that moves the key updates that module's fixture in the same commit, with a line "rewritten in v18, same concept". The review box stays, because the slot still tests the same idea.
- **`all`, `gap`, `order` and `match` go into the new test banks** (group 3), and a lesson may use them in its `pre`. Recall counts stay unchanged during the rewrite, so `index.js` (`meta.questions`) is not touched by parallel content tasks. A fifth recall slot of a new kind can come later, once the rollout is done.

### 2.4 Design: a question lint

A new pure module, `src/content/courses/lintquestions.js`, with a command-line entry like `validate.js`. It reports, per question: absolute words only in distractors; the key more than 1.3× longer than every other option; a distinctive stem word repeated only in the key; Gevorderd or Expert recall stems of 10 words or fewer that start with a definition verb; an option list with a "nobody does this" pattern from a small phrase list (geen verschil, negeren, niets doen, aantal slides, pagina’s). These are warnings, not validator errors, because a word list cannot judge a question.

`tests/questionlint.test.js` is a **ratchet**: per module, the warning count may not rise above a baseline fixture. Rewritten modules must reach zero warnings, or carry a written exception in the item (`lintOk: 'reason'`).

### 2.5 Design: the rewrite programme ("extra review of every question")

Every lesson's `pre` and `recall` is rewritten to the standard, module by module, in nl and en, by one content agent per module. A review agent then checks every item against the standard and against the lesson text. The review rubric: is the key correct? Is it unambiguous in the stated situation? Would a half-informed colleague pick each distractor? Does it fit the level? Is the `why` about the near miss? The same pass writes the module's **test bank and blueprint** (group 3) and its DMAIC references (group 1), so a module file is opened once. Projects modules go first (Merijn's focus), the harder ones within that early, then Engineering.

## Group 3: Practice tests

### 3.1 What exists today

- **Module check** (`src/core/modulecheck.js`, `src/ui/modulecheck.js`): 10 recall questions from the whole module, one pass, no hints. It opens once the module has bronze (every lesson passed), at most once a day. A perfect check with silver gives gold (`medalFor`, `src/core/flair.js`). Answers are scheduled; a right answer before its due date does not climb.
- **The learning path** (`src/ui/pathmap.js`, `topicRows`): per topic, its levels are stops on a line. A level shows once the one before it is passed. A finished topic ends in a flag. This is "zoals nu op de map te zien is".
- Nothing exists per topic, per level across a module, or across modules. Once all lessons are passed, `courseTodayPick` offers situations, say-it rounds, due reviews and one maintenance question a week. There is no larger test to keep training with.

### 3.2 Design

**Three test sizes, one engine** (`src/core/practicetest.js`, pure; screen `src/ui/practicetest.js`):

| Test | Content | Size | Unlocks (Q3 A) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Topic test** | all four levels of one topic, weighted to the higher levels | 12 | when the topic's flag shows (all four levels passed) |
| **Level test** of a module (Basis, Werkkennis, Gevorderd, Expert) | every topic of the module at levels ≤ N; half of the items at level N | 16–20 | when level N is passed in **every** topic of the module (the column of stops is complete on the path) |
| **Subject mix** per level | interleaved across every module whose level test N is open | 20 | once two or more modules have level test N open |

- **Sources.** The questions come from the module's **test bank** (hand-written, reviewed, all kinds; about 60%) plus the lessons' recall and pre questions (about 40%). Term questions are not used: they are too easy for a test.
- **Harder by construction.** Bank items are written at the top of their level: `best`, `all`, `order` and `match` with scenarios. A test does not repeat the items of the previous attempt while the pool allows it. Each attempt is seeded by its attempt number, so the tests vary.
- **Format.** One pass, no hints, no going back (the module check's `onePass`). A test may be stopped; it is then not scored. At the end: the score, a bar per topic or per level, every miss with its `why`, and a button "Oefen je zwakste onderwerp" that opens that topic test.
- **Passed** at 80%. The path shows a small test stop after the flag (topic test) and a row "Toetsen: Basis ✓ · Werkkennis 72% · Gevorderd (op slot) · Expert (op slot)" under the topics on the module screen.
- **Module check and gold (Q4 A).** The Expert level test becomes the module's final test and replaces the 10-question check. Gold is silver plus a final test of 90% or more. The old perfect check still counts, so nobody loses a medal.
- **Keep training after the modules.** In `courseTodayPick`, after the lesson steps, the next pick is `reason: 'test'`: the open test with the lowest last score, or the oldest attempt, at most one a day. The home's "Your next step" offers it.
- **Tests and reviews (Q5 A).** A recall question answered in a test counts as its review: a right answer on a question that is due climbs a box (a right answer before its due date does not, as in the module check); a miss goes to box 1. Bank items are **not** put on recurring review, so tests never grow the review pile. The tests themselves are the spaced practice of the bank.
- **Storage.** `state.courses[subject].tests[testId] = { best, last, attempts, lastDay, byTopic }`, with a test id like `pm.plan/level/2`, `pm.plan/topic/schedule` or `pm/mix/3`. `src/core/sync.js` gains `mergeTests` (the higher best, the later last, the higher attempt count) and a clean step, like `mergeChecks`. Sessions log `kind: 'test'`.

**The test bank** (the fixed base, also for the AI tests in 3.3):
- One file per module and language: `src/content/courses/banks/<moduleId>.<lang>.js`, exporting `{ items: [...] }`. Each item is a question of any kind, plus `id` (stable, `b<n>`, never reused), `lesson` (the lesson id whose level it tests) and `source` (the sentence of that lesson's text it rests on). The full question id is `<lessonId>#b<n>`, so `questionById`, `moduleOf` and `lessonOf` keep working.
- About 10 items per topic and level band (roughly 40 per module per language), at least three kinds per topic.
- **Blueprint**, one file per module: `src/content/courses/banks/<moduleId>.blueprint.js`. Per topic and level it holds `aims` (one to two lines on what the level tests, taken from the addendum-3 list) and `traps` (three to six real misconceptions, each usable as a distractor). The blueprint is the brief for the bank writer and the anchor for the AI generator.
- Validation: `checkBank` in a new file `src/content/courses/validatebank.js`, hooked into `runAll`. It checks the shared question rules, nl/en parity by `id`, that `lesson` is in the module, that `source` occurs in that lesson's text, that ids are unique, and that retired ids are not reused (a fixture of retired ids).
- Bank files are loaded with the module and are in `SHELL`, so tests work offline.

### 3.3 Design: AI-generated extra tests

- **Fixed base:** the reviewed bank and the blueprint of the topic, plus the lessons' own text. The generator may test only what the lesson text says.
- **Request** (`src/core/testgen.js`, using `callClaude`/`withFallbacks` from `src/core/claude.js` like `coursecoach.js`). Sent: the topic's lesson titles, teach blocks, terms and gists; the blueprint's aims and traps; three bank items as exemplars; the level mix. Nothing personal is sent. Structured JSON output in the same item schema, with a `source` sentence per item. Ten items per request, in the user's language and the settings model, at low effort.
- **Checked before shown, in three stages:**
  1. **Schema:** the browser runs the same `checkQuestion` and kind rules as the validator (`validate.js` is already loaded in the browser).
  2. **Local quality:** the lint rules of 2.4 must report zero; `source` must occur verbatim (after normalising) in the lesson text; the stem may not be a near-duplicate of a bank item or lesson question.
  3. **Independent check (Q8 A):** a second call gets the items without keys, plus the lesson text. It answers each item and judges two things: is exactly one answer (or one set or order) defensible from the text, and would any option be chosen by nobody? An item is kept only if the checker's answer equals the key, it is unambiguous, and it has no nonsense option.
  - If fewer than six items survive, the test is topped up from the bank, with the line "aangevuld met vaste vragen".
- **In the test.** Every generated item has a "Klopt niet" button. A flagged item is dropped from the score and kept in a local list ("Gemelde vragen" in Settings) with its text, so Merijn can pass it on for content review.
- **Generated items do not join recurring reviews (Q7 A).** A miss on a generated item brings the related lesson's recall questions forward, by making them due today.
- **Without a key** the extra-test button is not shown; the bank tests work as normal. **Offline with a key**, the last five generated sets per topic are cached on the device (localStorage, per device, not synced) and can be retaken. Otherwise the screen says "Offline: oefen met de vaste toets".

## Group 4: Review load (skip)

### 4.1 What exists today

- `courseTodayPick` (`src/core/course.js` line 412) makes "vragen ophalen" the next step whenever 5 or more questions are due. Lessons wait until the pile is cleared. A due session holds at most 10 questions from one track (`dueSession`); questions overdue by 60 days come back as lapsed, at most three per session. The due card on the course home (`src/ui/coursehome.js`) shows the full due count. There is no way to say "not now".

### 4.2 Design (Q6 A)

1. **"Vandaag overslaan"** as a secondary button on the due card and in the next-step card when the pick is `due`. It sets `state.courses[subject].dueSkip = today`. For the rest of that day `courseTodayPick` ignores the `due` reason, so the next step becomes the lesson or test. The due card turns quiet: "Overgeslagen tot morgen · 23 wachten", with a "Toch doen" link. Nothing changes in boxes or due dates: no penalty. Sync merges `dueSkip` as the later date.
2. **A daily limit** in Settings, `settings.reviewCap`: 10, 20 (default), 40 or no limit. `dueCount` shows at most the limit, and the due sessions of the day stop at the limit, counted from today's due sessions. The rest waits, oldest first. Because due dates are not changed, nothing is lost. If the waiting pile stays above three times the limit for a week, the guide says one line: "Je herhalingen stapelen op: verhoog je limiet of doe even minder nieuwe lessen."
3. **"Overslaan" per question** inside a due session (a ghost button): the question goes to the end of the session. If skipped again it is left out: not scheduled, still due tomorrow. Skipping is not a miss.

Tests (group 3) also lighten the pile, because recall questions answered there count as reviews (Q5).

## Group 5: Pictures instead of films

### 5.1 What exists today

- **Text-first films** (`src/content/courses/films/textfirst.js`, 17 Nov). 18 Projects films (39 lessons) are optional extras: the lesson runs Lezen · Voorbeeld and offers "Bekijk de film" in the first reading block. 21 Projects films (48 lessons) are the main step, as are all Engineering films, the module intros and the field films (`filmIsMain` and `lessonPhases` in `src/core/course.js`, `src/ui/film.js`). ### 5.2 Design: the rule

For each Projects lesson, ask where the reasoning happens:

1. **In a change over time** (something moves, fills, grows, flows or slips): **film**. Examples: critical path with a delay, Kanban flow, scope creep, ZOPA, cutover hour by hour.
2. **In a static structure** (a table, a matrix, a grid, a map, a cycle, a tree, a timeline at one moment, a comparison side by side): **picture with supporting text**. The text is the lesson's reading; every label on the picture is explained in the block next to it.
3. **In wording or conversation** (feedback, difficult talks, phrasing): **text only**.

**Engineering keeps its films** (Merijn). Pictures may be added there, but never replace a film. Module intro and field films stay as they are.

Applied:
- The 18 text-first films become picture lessons where a structure carries the lesson, for example pm.portfolio.benefits (output, outcome, benefit), pm.run.escalation (decision rights), pm.stuck.diagnose (the chain), pm.land.handover (the 12-week strip) and pm.outside.steering (milestones). Lessons that are pure wording stay text only: pm.lead.feedback, pm.lead.difficult, pm.outside.jargon.
- Projects lessons without a film get a picture where rule 2 holds. Candidates: the families table and the DMAIC route (group 1); risk 5×5, RACI, stakeholder grid, MoSCoW, the governance layers, OGSM.
- The three weak KEEPs in the 17 Nov triage (pm.plan.method, pm.plan.wbs, pm.devops.incident) are judged again by rule 2.
- Future Projects lesson films (Task 17 and beyond) are built only when rule 1 holds.
- **The film link (Q9 A):** a lesson that gets pictures in place of its text-first film keeps a small "Bekijk ook de film" link at the end of the reading. Nothing rendered is thrown away, but it is no longer offered in the first block.

### 5.3 Design: the data and the drawing

- **A lesson field** `pictures: [{ id: 'pm.approach.families.map', after: 0, alt: '…' }]`. `after` is a teach block index or `'example'`; at most two pictures per lesson; `alt` is at most 30 words, per language (each lesson file carries its own). The validator checks the id format, the range of `after`, the alt text, and that the picture exists.
- **Pictures are data, drawn by a small kit**, like the subject map (`src/ui/subjectmap.js`): `src/content/courses/pictures/<moduleId>.js` exports `{ [id]: { kind: 'table' | 'grid' | 'cycle' | 'flow' | 'tree' | 'timeline', ...labels in nl and en } }`, and `src/ui/picture.js` draws it as inline SVG. Colours come from CSS classes, so both themes work. The SVG has a `role="img"` and the alt text; it is phone-first (legible at 390 px) and works offline (the modules are in `SHELL`). No iframe and no extra page.
- **A test:** every label on a picture occurs in the lesson's text in the same language, so the supporting text really supports it.

## 4. Decisions for Merijn

The recommended option is listed first.

**Q1. How far does DMAIC go into the Projects structure?**
- A. The road becomes Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control; current modules regrouped under the phases; two new modules, Meten and Analyseren (Lean-oriented). *Most Lean and the clearest spine; costs two modules, a map change and two film re-renders.*
- B. The road gets DMAIC phase labels; Measure and Analyse become extra topics inside Intake and Plannen. *Cheaper, but those modules become crowded and the phases stay half-hidden.*
- C. Keep the structure; DMAIC only in the approach lessons and as references. *Least work; does not change how Projects feels.*

**Q2. Delivery projects (a line move, an ERP rollout) that are not improvement projects: which road?**
- A. One DMAIC spine for all, with a light Measure and Analyse for delivery work, and PRINCE2 or stage-gate as the governance layer over it. *One picture to learn; the lesson must explain where DMAIC fits less well.*
- B. Two roads: DMAIC for improvement, the current five stages for delivery projects. *More exact, but two structures on the map and in the films.*

**Q3. When do practice tests unlock?**
- A. A topic test at the topic's flag, and a level test per module once that level is passed in every topic. *Follows the path you see now; small and large tests both exist.*
- B. Only level tests per module. *Fewer, larger tests; nothing per topic.*
- C. Only topic tests. *Smaller tests; no test of a whole module.*

**Q4. What happens to the module check (10 questions) and the gold medal?**
- A. The Expert level test becomes the module's final test; gold at 90% or more. Old perfect checks still count. *One kind of test, harder gold.*
- B. Keep the module check next to the practice tests. *Nothing changes for medals; two similar things on one screen.*

**Q5. Do test answers count as reviews?**
- A. Yes. A recall question answered in a test counts as its review; bank questions never join the review pile. *Tests lighten the pile; a miss in a test brings the question back sooner.*
- B. No, tests stand apart from reviews. *Simpler to explain; you may answer the same question twice in a day.*

**Q6. How do you skip recurring reviews when there are too many?**
- A. "Vandaag overslaan" on the due card, a daily limit in Settings (default 20), and "overslaan" per question. *Full control and no penalty; three small controls.*
- B. Only "Vandaag overslaan". *The simplest; a large pile stays large.*
- C. Only the daily limit. *Calm by default; you cannot clear the day when you have no time.*

**Q7. Questions generated by AI: do they come back in your reviews?**
- A. No. They live only in that test; a miss brings the related lesson's own questions forward. *Only reviewed questions enter long-term memory.*
- B. Yes, a generated question you miss joins your reviews. *More practice on your weak spots; unreviewed text can stay with you.*

**Q8. How strictly are AI questions checked before you see them?**
- A. Local checks plus a second, independent AI call that answers each question blind; a question is kept only if it matches. *Fewer wrong or ambiguous questions; about twice the API cost and a few seconds more.*
- B. Local checks only. *Cheaper and faster; more wrong questions slip through (you can still flag them).*

**Q9. A lesson that gets pictures instead of its text-first film: what happens to the film?**
- A. A small "Bekijk ook de film" link stays at the end of the reading. *Nothing is lost; still a film in the lesson.*
- B. The film is no longer offered in the lesson. *Cleanest; the rendered films are no longer used there.*

**Q10. Questions with more than one right answer ("vink alle juiste aan"): how are they scored?**
- A. Only fully right counts for your progress; the review shows how many you had right. *Strict, matches "alle goede aanvinken"; partial knowledge is visible but not rewarded.*
- B. Partial credit counts: one wrong tick or one missing still climbs a box. *Kinder; makes these questions easier than intended.*
