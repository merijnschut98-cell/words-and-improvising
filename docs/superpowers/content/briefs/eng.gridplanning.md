# eng.gridplanning — Grid planning and the energy transition (engineering, track grid, level 4, FADED examples)
Index title: en 'Grid planning and the transition' / nl 'Netplanning en de energietransitie'. Blurb: en 'Expert level: load flow, N-1, flexibility, batteries and how a grid operator decides what to build.' / nl 'Expertniveau: loadflow, N-1, flexibiliteit, batterijen en hoe een netbeheerder kiest wat er gebouwd wordt.'
1. `eng.gridplanning.loadflow` — load flow in words: voltage drop, loading, why a cable can be full while the station has room.
2. `eng.gridplanning.redundancy` — N-1 and redundancy: what it guarantees, what it costs, when operators relax it.
3. `eng.gridplanning.flex` — flexibility: curtailment, non-firm contracts, time-dependent capacity, congestion management in practice.
4. `eng.gridplanning.storage` — batteries and storage on the grid: where they help, where they make congestion worse.
5. `eng.gridplanning.investment` — investment planning: scenarios, prioritising expansions, lead times of substations and cables.
6. `eng.gridplanning.execution` — from plan to built grid: capacity of the work chain, standardisation, contractors, permits; where improvement work helps most.

Facts and recalculations:
- I = S / (√3 × U): 2,500 / (1.732 × 10) = 144.3 A; 3,000 / 17.32 = 173.2 A; 210 + 144 = 354 A = 118% of 300 A; 340 / 400 = 85%. Aluminium MS cable 250–300 A ≈ 4.3–5.2 MVA at 10 kV (rule of thumb, depends on type and laying).
- LS voltage band 230 V ±10% (207–253 V) as 10-minute averages (Netcode / EN 50160); inverters trip on sustained overvoltage around 253 V.
- N-1: 2 × 40 MVA → firm ≈ 40; 46 MVA on one unit = 115%; 46 − 40 = 6 over N-1; 80 − 46 = 34 idle; +2 MVA/yr × 3 = 52; three 25 MVA units → 50 firm. “Emergency lane” (vluchtstrook) attributed to TenneT. Netcode named as ACM’s technical rules.
- Flex: 8,760 h a year; 70% solar connection (Netbeheer Nederland practice, energy loss a few percent, about 3%); 5.8 + 1.5 = 7.3 MW; 4.2 + 1.5 = 5.7 MW; 10 MWp × 0.7 = 7 MW. GOPACS: platform of the Dutch TSO and DSOs (2019) for redispatch (buy inside, sell outside). Congestion-management study before refusal (Netcode congestion-management rules, ACM 2022). Group contract (groepstransportovereenkomst) mentioned without detail.
- Storage: 20 MWh / 10 MW = 2 h; 40 / 20 = 2 h; 50 kW × 4 h = 200 kWh; village example 460 − 400 = 60 kW × 3 h = 180 kWh; +20 kVA/yr × 3 → 120 kW × 3 h = 360 kWh above 300 kWh (assumption added that the 3-hour window stays). Time-duration-bound transport right: at least 85% of the hours of a year, so up to 0.15 × 8,760 = 1,314 h limited; started as TenneT’s ATR85 pilot, later an ACM-codified product. EU Electricity Directive 2019/944 (art. 36 and 54): grid operators may not own or operate storage except under narrow exceptions.
- Investment: 2033 − 8 = 2025. II3050 is Netbeheer Nederland’s joint scenario study; RES = 30 regional energy strategies; investment plans every two years. ACM prioritisation since 2024 (congestion relievers, then safety, then basic needs; default first come, first served); details revised since, so the text says to check the current framework.
- Execution: 10 × 2 × 44 = 880; 1 + 0.3 × 0.5 = 1.15; 880 / 1.15 = 765.2; at 15% rework 880 / 1.075 = 818.6 ≈ 820 (the reveal now shows this sum); 8 × 3 × 45 = 1,080.

Uncertain: the exact ACM decision that turned ATR85 into a standard product (text says “later”, no year); ACM’s position on anticipatory investment is stated generally (“may do this when it follows from plausible scenarios”); the current wording of the prioritisation categories (examples only); Dutch SAIDI and queue sizes are not used in this module.
