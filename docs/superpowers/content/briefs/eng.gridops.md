# eng.gridops — Grid operations and asset management (engineering, track grid, level 3, FADED examples)
Index title: en 'Grid operations and assets' / nl 'Netbedrijf en assetmanagement'. Blurb: en 'Outages, switching plans, asset management and the energy-transition workload at a grid operator.' / nl 'Storingen, schakelplannen, assetmanagement en de werkvoorraad van de energietransitie bij een netbeheerder.'
1. `eng.gridops.outage` — outage handling: detection, the control room, isolation, restore by switching, repair; SAIDI/CAIDI.
2. `eng.gridops.switching` — switching plans: purpose, steps, approvals, why they are a bottleneck in planned work.
3. `eng.gridops.assets` — asset management: risk = likelihood × consequence, ISO 55000 in one idea, replacement decisions.
4. `eng.gridops.data` — asset data quality, condition data, digital twins; why bad data stalls planning.
5. `eng.gridops.workload` — energy-transition workload: more work than technicians, prioritisation, bundling work, standard designs.
6. `eng.gridops.chain` — the work chain from request to commissioning: work preparation, materials, permits, crews, where flow breaks.

Facts and recalculations:
- Five safety rules (EN 50110-1 / NEN 3140 / NEN 3840): disconnect; secure against reconnection (lock and tag); verify absence of voltage with a tested detector; earth and short-circuit, only after the test; protect against adjacent live parts. Restore in reverse. NEN 3140 low voltage, NEN 3840 high voltage (above 1 kV AC, so MS included). Written appointment by the employer for those who switch.
- SAIDI = SAIFI × CAIDI; recall 1,600,000 / 100,000 = 16 min; CAIDI 80 → 100 at equal SAIFI raises SAIDI by 25%. Dutch SAIDI “roughly 20 to 30 minutes a year” kept as a range (Netbeheer Nederland, Betrouwbaarheid van elektriciteitsnetten in Nederland, yearly; recent years around 20–25 min); ACM’s quality term (q-factor) in the regional operators’ revenue regulation.
- Switching example: 6 × 2 × 4 × 12 = 576 slots; 700 × 2 = 1,400; 1,400 / 576 = 2.43. Fixed the en reveal: “bundling pairs saves a slot pair per job” now says each pair of jobs sharing one outage saves 2 slots (2 instead of 4), matching the nl.
- Assets: 0.02 × 2,000 × 180 = 7,200; 0.2 × 40 × 240 = 1,920; recall 0.05 × 1,200 × 120 = 7,200. NTA 8120 named as the Dutch asset-management certification for grid operators (builds on ISO 55000/55001).
- Data: 250 × 0.5 = 125 days (about 200 working days in an FTE year, so more than half); recall 800 × 0.3 × 4 = 960 h.
- Workload: 120 × 1,300 = 156,000 h; 210,000 − 156,000 = 54,000 (25.7%); 50% → 60% hands-on is +20% = 31,200 h; recall 80 × 1,200 = 96,000 = 80% of 120,000.
- ACM prioritisation (codebesluit prioriteringsruimte bij transportverzoeken, 2024): default first come, first served; congestion relievers first, then safety (e.g. defence, emergency services), then basic needs (e.g. health care, education, housing). Categories stated as examples because ACM has revised the framework since.
- Chain: 8 + 3 + 9 + 13 + 1 = 34; parallel 8 + 3 + 13 + 1 = 25; recall 4 + 12 + 2 = 18 vs 28. Added: the 18-week connection term for small connections (old Elektriciteitswet/Netcode) is dropped in the Energiewet, in force 1 January 2026.

Uncertain: the size of the wachtrij is stated only as “many thousands of businesses” in 2025 (Netbeheer Nederland capacity map); the technician shortage only as “thousands”; what replaces the 18-week term in detail (the text only says a date now depends on the work and the queue); privacy limits on smart-meter data stated generally (use mostly aggregated per street or station).

Facts and sources (checked 2026-11-09):
- Statutory 18-week term (Electricity Act 1998, connections up to 10 MVA) applied until 21 February 2025, then a “reasonable term”; reason: CJEU case law that the regulator, not the legislator, sets such terms (Loyens & Loeff, “De aansluittermijn: van harde deadline naar redelijke termijn”; Dirkzwager). The Energy Act keeps the reasonable term.
- Small connections: ACM code decision of 2023 (12/18/52 weeks) annulled by the CBb; in disputes the ACM starts from 18–22 weeks; a code change with concrete terms is in preparation (ACM dispute decisions Teunissen/Enexis, 10 June 2025, and WoonFriesland/Liander, 16 July 2026). Offer within 10 working days of a complete request (Grid Code art. 8.3(g), cited in the 2025 decision; Enexis breached it with an offer after almost four months).
- Large connections: ACM code decision of 25 March 2024 (published 18 April 2024), terms since 1 January 2025, now in the Electricity System Code: 26 or 52 weeks after a complexity analysis, or an operator-set term that cannot reasonably be shorter; plus a regional waiting time per quarter from the stock of work, capped at 40 weeks, cap running down over ten years (acm.nl “Codebesluit aansluittermijnen grote aansluitingen elektriciteit”; wording aligned with eng.transformers). System Code in force 21 February 2026 (as eng.gridbasics).
- Planned interruption notice: at least 3 working days for small consumers, 3 or 10 working days for large. Compensation: up to 3 × 25 A €35 for 4–8 h + €20 per further full 4 h; 3 × 35 to 3 × 80 A €195 + €100; paid within 6 months unasked; not for planned work (acm.nl “Vergoeding stroomstoring en gasstoring”; Stedin compensatieregeling “na 8 uur en vervolgens voor elke 4 uur meer”; the 13th hour is not a full period, Gaslicht).
- National outage number 0800-9009, free, 24/7, routes to the regional operator (Gaslicht, Selectra).
- Outage minutes 2024: unplanned 23.9 = 0.343 × 69.8 min; planned 23.3 (Netbeheer Nederland, Betrouwbaarheid van elektriciteitsnetten in Nederland, resultaten 2024; as eng.reliability).
- Investment plans 2025: up to €15 bn a year (electricity and gas); 400 of just over 900 larger electricity projects later than planned (Netbeheer Nederland via Solar Magazine, 3 Nov 2025).
- Technicians: about 30,000 more technicians for operators and contractors by 2030 (Netbeheer Nederland, Stand van de uitvoering Q1 2025; other sources say 28,000 to 2029); Opschalingsplan 2030 to recruit 5,000 extra with €25 m (Nov 2025). Hedged.
- GOPACS: joint platform of TenneT and the regional operators, launched 2019; operators place requests daily; a buy order in the area is matched with an opposite order outside so the balance is kept; the operator pays the price difference; connected platforms ETPA and EPEX SPOT (ETPA knowledge hub and GOPACS FAQ; Solar Magazine). Redispatch only via a CSP (Netbeheer Nederland infographic congestiemanagement, Oct 2024).
- Congestion study and participation: > 500 kW invited; mandatory from 1 MW in a congested area (operator sets the bound); ≥ 60 MW contracted always, inside and outside congested areas; vital infrastructure exempt; producers 3 months, consumers 6 months to offer; regulated fee when prices are unreasonably high (same infographic). Taught as “according to Netbeheer Nederland; the rules are in the System Code”.
- Capacity-steering contract (CSC) since December 2025: the operator may also ask for more use in the direction opposite to the congestion (ACM codebesluit capaciteitssturingscontract; Solar Magazine). ACM binding agreements with all seven operators to use the grid better (Solar Magazine, June 2026). Both hedged (“check the current code”).
- WIBON as the law behind the KLIC notification: general knowledge, not separately sourced.

## Review of v13 topics (2026-11-09)

Corrections (supersede the notes above where they differ):
- Regional waiting time for large connections: capped at 40 weeks in 2025, the cap falling two weeks a year to 20 weeks at the end of 2034 (temporary scheme to 31 Dec 2034; ACM explanatory note to the working group, Solar Magazine “Netbeheerders verschaffen duidelijkheid over aansluittermijnen grootverbruikers”). It does not run down to zero. Taught as “decided by the ACM in March 2024, applying since 1 January 2025”.
- Planned-interruption notice: small connections (up to 3 × 80 A) at least 3 working days, large connections on a distribution grid at least 10 working days, transmission connections a yearly outage plan (ACM code proposal “onderhoudsvergoeding N-1”, 10 June 2026). “3 or 10 for large” removed.
- CSC: ACM code decision 11 Dec 2025 (published 12 Dec 2025); since May 2026 it has largely replaced the CBC (RVO; ACM draft: the CBC becomes a variant of the CSC). Aligned word for word with eng.gridplanning (term “Capacity steering contract”, CBC and CSC definitions).
- Prioritisation categories in the revised framework (ACM, 12 Dec 2025): safety now includes hospitals and emergency services; basic needs are housing and education.
- Mandatory participation: an obliged customer still sets its own price, but in principle offers all its contracted capacity; a consumer that cannot must explain in writing (Netbeheer Nederland infographic, Oct 2024).
- Outage compensation: exclusions are planned work and some exceptional situations (ACM).
- Shared definitions re-copied after main v136: Congestion management, GOPACS (eng.gridbasics/eng.gridplanning), Normally open point / Normaal openpunt (eng.gridbasics), Five safety rules (eng.switchgear). Risk / Risico has no counterpart in eng.reliability (only Risk matrix and Risk in euros); kept.
- Answer lengths: the correct option was almost never the longest (4/64 en, 1/64 nl), which is a giveaway in reverse. Longest distractors shortened in 16 questions; length rank now en 15 · 12 · 18 · 19, nl 12 · 15 · 17 · 20; positions unchanged (16 each, en = nl).
