Facts and recalculations:
- Five safety rules (EN 50110-1 / NEN 3140 / NEN 3840): disconnect; secure against reconnection (lock and tag); verify absence of voltage with a tested detector; earth and short-circuit, only after the test; protect against adjacent live parts. Restore in reverse. NEN 3140 low voltage, NEN 3840 high voltage (above 1 kV AC, so MS included). Written appointment by the employer for those who switch.
- SAIDI = SAIFI × CAIDI; recall 1,600,000 / 100,000 = 16 min; CAIDI 80 → 100 at equal SAIFI raises SAIDI by 25%. Dutch SAIDI “roughly 20 to 30 minutes a year” kept as a range (Netbeheer Nederland, Betrouwbaarheid van elektriciteitsnetten in Nederland, yearly; recent years around 20–25 min); ACM’s quality term (q-factor) in the regional operators’ revenue regulation.
- Switching example: 6 × 2 × 4 × 12 = 576 slots; 700 × 2 = 1,400; 1,400 / 576 = 2.43. Fixed the en reveal: “bundling pairs saves a slot pair per job” now says each pair of jobs sharing one outage saves 2 slots (2 instead of 4), matching the nl.
- Assets: 0.02 × 2,000 × 180 = 7,200; 0.2 × 40 × 240 = 1,920; recall 0.05 × 1,200 × 120 = 7,200. NTA 8120 named as the Dutch asset-management certification for grid operators (builds on ISO 55000/55001).
- Data: 250 × 0.5 = 125 days (about 200 working days in an FTE year, so more than half); recall 800 × 0.3 × 4 = 960 h.
- Workload: 120 × 1,300 = 156,000 h; 210,000 − 156,000 = 54,000 (25.7%); 50% → 60% hands-on is +20% = 31,200 h; recall 80 × 1,200 = 96,000 = 80% of 120,000.
- ACM prioritisation (codebesluit prioriteringsruimte bij transportverzoeken, 2024): default first come, first served; congestion relievers first, then safety (e.g. defence, emergency services), then basic needs (e.g. health care, education, housing). Categories stated as examples because ACM has revised the framework since.
- Chain: 8 + 3 + 9 + 13 + 1 = 34; parallel 8 + 3 + 13 + 1 = 25; recall 4 + 12 + 2 = 18 vs 28. Added: the 18-week connection term for small connections (old Elektriciteitswet/Netcode) is dropped in the Energiewet, in force 1 January 2026.

Uncertain: the size of the wachtrij is stated only as “many thousands of businesses” in 2025 (Netbeheer Nederland capacity map); the technician shortage only as “thousands”; what replaces the 18-week term in detail (the text only says a date now depends on the work and the queue); privacy limits on smart-meter data stated generally (use mostly aggregated per street or station).
