# pm.close — Close (projecten, track project, level 2)
Index title: en 'Close' / nl 'Afsluiten'. Blurb: en 'Finish well: prove results, close loose ends, learn, and leave the client stronger.' / nl 'Goed afronden: resultaat aantonen, losse eindjes sluiten, leren, en de klant sterker achterlaten.'
1. `pm.close.results` — proving results: baseline vs after, separating your effect from noise, money in the client’s terms.
2. `pm.close.acceptance` — formal acceptance: criteria agreed at the start, sign-off, open points list.
3. `pm.close.retro` — retrospective/evaluation that people learn from: format, safety, turning lessons into actions.
4. `pm.close.report` — the closing report and presentation: one page, the story, what’s next.
5. `pm.close.followup` — follow-up and the next project: account management without being pushy.
6. `pm.close.yourself` — your own reflection: what you’d do differently, collecting feedback, building your case history.

### Facts and sources (v13)
- Run chart rules (shift ≥ 6 on one side of the median, points on the median skipped; trend ≥ 5 rising/falling; astronomical point; runs rule): Perla, Provost & Murray, “The run chart: a simple analytical tool for learning from variation in healthcare processes”, BMJ Qual Saf 20 (2011) 46–51. Thresholds vary by source (8 for long series), stated as conventions; “about ten points” hedged as often said.
- Difference-in-differences: Card & Krueger (1994), “Minimum Wages and Employment”, American Economic Review 84(4) (NJ vs PA fast food). Parallel-trends assumption standard.
- Hawthorne effect: Western Electric experiments, 1920s; Levitt & List (2011), “Was There Really a Hawthorne Effect at the Hawthorne Plant?”, AEJ: Applied 3(1) 224–38 — original illumination data show no systematic pattern; stated as disputed.
- Sunk costs, escalation of commitment (Arkes & Blumer 1985; Staw 1976) and kill criteria are taught in pm.run.rescope (the decision whether to stop). pm.close.stop starts after the decision: premature closure, taking stock, safe stopping point, early termination clause, sunk costs only as a trap when choosing which half-done work to finish; it points to pm.run.rescope. Annie Duke, Quit (2022): most people quit too late.
- PRINCE2 Closing a Project (prepare planned / premature closure, hand over products, evaluate the project, recommend closure; the board authorises closure in Directing a Project; follow-on action recommendations; lessons report; “capture previous lessons” in Starting up a Project): AXELOS/PeopleCert manuals; checked via https://prince2.wiki/processes/closing-a-project. PMBOK “Close Project or Phase”.
- Dutch retention duty for financial records 7 years: art. 52 lid 4 AWR (Algemene wet inzake rijksbelastingen, “General State Taxes Act”), checked on wetten.overheid.nl. GDPR storage limitation: art. 5(1)(e) GDPR.
- Five retrospective phases: Derby & Larsen, Agile Retrospectives (2006). ROTI 0–4 scale as one variant (1–5 also used). 4Ls credited to Mary Gorman & Ellen Gottesdiener (EBG Consulting).
- Terry Williams (2008), “How Do Organizations Learn Lessons From Projects—And Do They?”, IEEE Trans. Eng. Mgmt 55(2) 248–266 — summarised as “recording usually works, dissemination and embedding are the weak point” (hedged “in short”). Argyris & Schön (1978), Organizational Learning: single/double-loop. Community of practice (Lave & Wenger).
- NPS: Reichheld, “The One Number You Need to Grow”, HBR Dec 2003; promoters 9–10, passives 7–8, detractors 0–6. Critique: Keiningham, Cooil, Andreassen & Aksoy (2007), Journal of Marketing 71(3) 39–51 (Norwegian data, no superiority).
- Block, Flawless Consulting (1st ed. 1981): last phase extension, recycle or termination. Schein, Process Consultation (1969). Mazzucato & Collington, The Big Con (2023), stated as an argument the industry disputes.
