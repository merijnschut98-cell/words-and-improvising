# eng.transformers — Transformers, cables and connections (engineering, track grid, level 2)
Index title: en 'Transformers and cables' / nl 'Transformatoren en kabels'. Blurb: en 'How transformers and cables work, how they age and fail, and what a new connection really takes.' / nl 'Hoe transformatoren en kabels werken, hoe ze verouderen en falen, en wat een nieuwe aansluiting echt vraagt.'
1. `eng.transformers.principle` — turns ratio, rated power (kVA/MVA), losses, cooling (ONAN), tap changer.
2. `eng.transformers.ageing` — loading and temperature, insulation ageing, oil analysis (DGA) as a health check.
3. `eng.transformers.cables` — MS and LS cable types (XLPE, paper-insulated), joints and terminations, typical failure causes.
4. `eng.transformers.faults` — cable faults: digging damage, joint failures; locating faults; repair time.
5. `eng.transformers.connections` — connection process: request, capacity check, design, work preparation, execution; lead times.
6. `eng.transformers.standard` — standardisation: standard substations and connection types, why standards speed up the work.

Facts and sources added or checked
- Three-phase current I = S / (√3 × U): 630 kVA → 909 A at 400 V, 36.4 A at 10 kV; 400 kVA → 577 A at 400 V, 23.1 A at 10 kV.
- Turns ratio vs voltage ratio: a Dyn5 transformer (HV delta, LV star with neutral) has turns per winding 10,000 / (400/√3) = 43.3; the rated voltage ratio is 25. Recall r2 now states the assumption that both windings are connected the same way (turns ratio = voltage ratio = 50).
- Losses: EU ecodesign regulation 548/2014 (amended 2019/1783), Tier 2 since July 2021, 630 kVA ≤ 24 kV: Pk ≤ 5,400 W, P0 ≤ 540 W (A0 − 10%) → “roughly 0.5 kW no-load, about 5 kW at full load”; efficiency at rated load, pf 1: 630 / (630 + 5.9) ≈ 99.1%. Hum at 100 Hz: magnetostriction at twice the 50 Hz grid frequency.
- Tap changer: off-circuit taps usually ±2 × 2.5% (5 positions); one step = 2.5% × 230 V = 5.75 V ≈ 6 V. Voltage limit 230 V + 10% = 253 V (EN 50160 / Netcode).
- Ageing: IEC 60076-7 (loading guide): relative ageing V = 2^((θh − 98)/6) for non-upgraded paper; design hot spot 98 °C; 12 °C → 4×. Life 40–60 years hedged as “can last”. Oil time constant ~180 min (IEC 60076-7 ONAN default; same as the figure).
- DGA gas interpretation: IEC 60599 / IEEE C57.104 (H2 partial discharge, C2H2 arcing, C2H4 thermal, CO paper).
- Cables: PILC (GPLK = gepantserde papier-loodkabel); XLPE in Dutch MV since the 1980s–90s; water treeing in early XLPE. Drum lengths “a few hundred metres up to about a kilometre” (hedged; depends on cable size). 2,400 / 600 = 4 lengths, 3 joints; 3,000 / 500 = 6 lengths, 5 joints.
- Faults: TDR pulse velocity in MV cable ~150–170 m/µs; 160 × 10 / 2 = 800 m. KLIC notification: required before mechanical digging under the WIBON act, maps from the Kadaster (recall r4 sharpened to “mechanical digging”).
- Connections: 3 × 80 A × 230 V = 55.2 kVA. The fixed 18-week term was removed from art. 23 Elektriciteitswet 1998 per 22 February 2025; the Energiewet (in force 1 January 2026, art. 3.38) only asks for a reasonable term. ACM code decision for large connections (Netcode elektriciteit art. 8.13, in force 1 January 2025): 26 weeks low complexity, 52 weeks medium, high complexity no fixed term, plus a capped dynamic waiting time (38 weeks for 2026). The small-connection code decision (12/18/52 weeks, 2023) was annulled by the CBb on 17 September 2024. Source: Loyens & Loeff, “De aansluittermijn: van harde deadline naar redelijke termijn” (2025). Example 2 + 1 + 3 + 8 + 4 = 18 weeks; recall 2 + 3 + 8 + 4 = 17.
- Standardisation: 300 × (40 − 8) = 9,600 h ≈ 6 FTE at ~1,600 productive h/year (assumption now stated); 250 × 30 = 7,500 h. Transformer delivery times of a year or more since ~2021: widely reported (IEA, US DOE/NREL supply-chain reports), hedged as “often”.

Uncertain / simplified: “often 10 to 20” secondary substations per ring, the 120% one-hour example, and the “few hundred kVA” MV threshold are rough practitioner figures, worded as such. “Streng” for an MV feeder is common DSO usage, but some colleagues say “MS-veld” or “uitgaande kabel”.

Facts and sources (checked 2026-11-08/09):
- Ecodesign Regulation (EU) 548/2014, amended by (EU) 2019/1783; Tier 1 from 1 July 2015, Tier 2 from 1 July 2021. Tier 2, liquid-immersed ≤ 24 kV: 400 kVA Ak 3,250 W / A0−10% 387 W; 630 kVA 4,600 W / 540 W; 1,000 kVA 7,600 W / 693 W; Tier 1 630 kVA Ck 6,500 W / A0 600 W (Siemens Energy Ecodesign leaflet; SGB-SMIT Tier 2 brochure; corroborated by a Croatian journal paper on a 630 kVA Tier 2 unit, hrcak.srce.hr). Correction to the clarity-round note: Tier 2 Pk for 630 kVA is 4,600 W, not 5,400 W; the principle lesson now says "at most 0.54 kW no-load and 4.6 kW full load". The amendment's exception for replacements in existing stations is described only loosely ("would not fit an existing station at reasonable cost").
- IEC 60076-7 loading guide: V = 2^((θh − 98)/6); limits for distribution transformers: normal cyclic 1.5 pu, hot spot 120 °C, top oil 105 °C; long-time emergency 1.8 pu, 140 °C, 115 °C; bubble risk above ≈140 °C. Same rule and wording as eng.reliability.
- DGA: IEC 60599, IEEE C57.104-2019 (levels, rates, Duval triangle 1 with zones PD, D1, D2, T1 below 300 °C, T2 300–700 °C, T3 above 700 °C, DT). The example 30/65/5% lies in T3 (C2H4 > 50%, C2H2 < 15%). DP ≈1,000–1,200 new, ≈200 at end of life (common literature values).
- Short-circuit impedance 4% up to 630 kVA, 6% above (EN 50588-1 / HD 428 typical values; taught as "typical").
- Enexis tender: Solar Magazine, 2 April 2026, "Enexis kiest 5 leveranciers voor jaarlijkse levering van 2.700 distributietransformatoren" (17 registrations, 12 in the award phase, 11 full bids; criteria energy losses, sustainability and material use, supply chain, control & traceability, TCO; multi-source strategy). No contract length or lead time is given there.
- Lead times: Wood Mackenzie (2024: ≈120 weeks average for transformers in the US, up from ≈50 in 2021; Q2 2025 power transformers ≈128 weeks; US distribution transformers back to ≈30 weeks after a 100+ week peak in 2023); an IEA-attributed 48–60 months for large power transformers in Europe (not verified). Taught hedged: "for distribution transformers often a year or more at the peak, for large power transformers two to four years; figures move every year". No current Dutch DSO figure was found; gridops keeps its "30-week delivery time" line as a scenario.
- II3050 / Integrale Infrastructuurverkenning 2030–2050 (2023), as reported by Solar Magazine: ≈50,000 new transformer stations and 100,000 km cable by 2050. ACM warning that operators cannot carry out all planned investments on time (Solar Magazine report on the ACM).
- Utilities Directive 2014/25/EU, implemented in the Aanbestedingswet 2012 (part 3, special sectors). Threshold not quoted.
- Smart Cable Guard: developed by KEMA (now DNV), online PD and fault location with sensors at both cable ends, used by Dutch DSOs (DNV product material; wording kept general). VLF 0.1 Hz (IEEE 400.2). XLPE 90 °C continuous (IEC 60502); cable rating per IEC 60287.
- Natural ester: IEC 62770; fire point above 300 °C (K-class); slower paper ageing in ester (IEEE C57.154).
- CNAIM (Ofgem); NTA 8120; Nestor (Dutch outage registration kept by the grid operators).
- Systeemcode elektriciteit 2026 and Energiewet: as in the eng.gridbasics brief (ACM codebesluit 5 Feb 2026).
