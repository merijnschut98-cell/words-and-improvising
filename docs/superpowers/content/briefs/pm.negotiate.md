# pm.negotiate — Negotiating and holding your ground (projecten, track project, level 3, FADED examples, pushback on every lesson)
Index title: en 'Negotiating and holding your ground' / nl 'Onderhandelen en voet bij stuk houden'. Blurb: en 'Interests, alternatives, first numbers, scope with a client, saying no, and disagreeing with someone more senior.' / nl 'Belangen, alternatieven, het eerste bod, scope met een klant, nee zeggen, en het oneens zijn met iemand die hoger staat.'
Serves assertiveness and client work for a junior consultant. Not Lean, Kata or A3.
1. `pm.negotiate.interests` — positions vs interests; questions that uncover interests; trades from different interests; fair standards.
2. `pm.negotiate.batna` — your alternative and theirs: BATNA, walk-away point, ZOPA; improving your BATNA; no bluffing.
3. `pm.negotiate.anchor` — first numbers, anchoring and framing; responding when they anchor first; loss framing, honestly.
4. `pm.negotiate.scope` — negotiating scope and time with a client: the trade between scope, time and cost; change log; options; check with your own manager.
5. `pm.negotiate.no` — saying no and offering a trade: Ury’s Yes–No–Yes; one reason; what is never traded (safety, integrity).
6. `pm.negotiate.senior` — disagreeing with someone more senior, calmly: fact, view, request; setting; when to commit and when to escalate.

The 7 Rules of Power: Merijn uses four writing principles drawn from Pfeffer (confident framing, no apologetic hedging, appreciation without deference, efficiency over explanation). They are taught in assert (with Erickson et al. on powerless speech as evidence that hedges cost credibility) and applied in price (writing the fee) and senior (pointer). The rules themselves are presented as Pfeffer’s practitioner argument, with the critique that they can reward self-promotion; pm.outside.decide already cites the same book for announcing decisions.
