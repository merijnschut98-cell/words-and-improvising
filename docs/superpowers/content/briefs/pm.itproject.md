# pm.itproject — Leading IT projects (projecten, track agile, level 3)

Index title: en 'Leading IT projects' / nl 'IT-projecten leiden'. The fourth module of the Projecten track `agile` (plan: docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-07-v13-listen-ict-outside-layout.md, section 3), placed after pm.devops in index.js. Films planned on these lessons: `pm.itproject.mvp`, `pm.itproject.cutover`, `pm.itproject.uat` (v14 plan §3.9); no `film` field yet.

Format: topic module, 4 topics × 4 levels, as pm.devops. Worked examples at levels 1–2, faded at 3–4. Every lesson: 1 pre question, 5 teach blocks ≤ 90 words, a `said` line, 4 recall questions (one numeric), a situation with pushback, 4–6 terms in the same order in en and nl, each defined once in the module. Clients: Dutch manufacturers (food, meat, dairy, plastics, metal, cable, packaging, furniture, bakery) and grid operators.

## Facts and sources (checked 2026-11-13)

- MoSCoW: Dai Clegg, Oracle, 1994 (Clegg & Barker, *Case Method Fast-Track: A RAD Approach*, 1994), later central to DSDM: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MoSCoW_method . The lessons say “devised in 1994 at Oracle … later a fixed part of DSDM”; they do not claim DSDM itself dates MoSCoW to 1994 (Wikipedia: used extensively with DSDM from 2002).
- DSDM / Agile Business Consortium, MoSCoW prioritisation: https://www.agilebusiness.org/dsdm-project-framework/moscow-prioritisation.html — Must = “Minimum Usable SubseT”; “typically no more than 60% Must Have effort”; “typically around 20% Could Have effort” as contingency; Won’t Have “will not be delivered (as part of this timeframe)”, recorded to prevent informal reintroduction. No Should percentage is given; the lessons give none.
- MVP: term coined by Frank Robinson (SyncDev) in 2001; popularised by Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup* (2011), as the version that yields the most validated learning: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_viable_product (secondary sources agree; Robinson’s 2001 text not seen). The looser ERP/MES use is stated as such.
- SAP Activate: phases Discover, Prepare, Explore, Realize, Deploy, Run; fit-to-standard workshops in Explore, standard shown in a sandbox / starter system, gaps to a backlog: https://learning.sap.com/courses/discovering-sap-activate-implementation-tools-and-methodology/describing-the-methodology-structure and https://www.sap.com/products/erp/activate-methodology.html . The lesson names the five core phases from Prepare.
- Acceptance testing: ISTQB glossary (older edition) “formal testing with respect to user needs, requirements, and business processes”, as quoted in https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acceptance_testing ; UAT by intended end users or subject-matter experts (same page).
- Hershey, 1999: the new ERP went live as Halloween orders came in; problems kept Hershey “from delivering $100 million worth of Kisses and Jolly Ranchers for Halloween that year” (CIO, “Hershey’s bittersweet lesson”): https://www.cio.com/article/270245/supply-chain-management-supply-chain-hershey-s-bittersweet-lesson.html . The lesson does not claim big bang or testing shortcuts (CIO does not say so).
- GDPR article 28: processing by a processor must be governed by a contract (verwerkersovereenkomst). Stated without further detail.
- Grid operators and procurement: they tender large contracts under procurement law (Aanbestedingswet 2012 part 3, special sectors; some contracts under the ADV from 2026 with the Energiewet). The lesson only says they “must follow procurement rules for large contracts: criteria fixed and published up front”; no thresholds.
- Hypercare 2–6 weeks: taken from pm.land.handover, not restated as a new fact.
- Contract wording: “nacalculatie” kept because pm.intake and pm.consult already use it; the lesson adds that IT also calls it “regie” (time and materials). Decommissioning in Dutch: “uitfaseren”, with the old system on “alleen-lezen” (read-only).
- Rules of thumb stated as such, without sources: severity scale 1–4, “95% passed” exit criterion, cutover plans of 200–400 lines, a post-go-live performance dip, key users spending a large part of their week.
