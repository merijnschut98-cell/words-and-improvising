# pm.stuck — When you don’t know what to do (projecten, track project, level 3, FADED examples)
Index title: en 'When you’re stuck' / nl 'Als je vastzit'. Blurb: en 'What to do when you don’t know: diagnosis, buying time, asking for help and acting under uncertainty.' / nl 'Wat je doet als je het niet weet: diagnose, tijd kopen, hulp vragen en handelen onder onzekerheid.'
1. `pm.stuck.diagnose` — naming the problem: is it unclear goal, unclear way, people, or capacity? A quick diagnostic.
2. `pm.stuck.buytime` — buying time professionally: “let me come back to you on Thursday”, holding statements, not bluffing.
3. `pm.stuck.askhelp` — asking for help well: whom, how to frame it, the senior colleague and the client as resources.
4. `pm.stuck.experiment` — acting under uncertainty: small experiments, reversible vs irreversible decisions.
5. `pm.stuck.pressure` — when the client pushes or escalates: staying calm, facts first, the steering group as ally.
6. `pm.stuck.reset` — resetting a failing project: honest assessment, the reset conversation, re-contracting.

Pressure is a political situation, so it sits at level 2 under politics. Reset is the core of restarting and becomes level 3, with recovery (what happens after the reset conversation) at level 4. Facts and sources (checked 2026-11-08):
- Last Planner System and PPC = completed / planned commitments per week, binary — G. Ballard, PhD thesis (UC Berkeley, 2000); Lean Construction Institute LPS workbook (https://leanconstruction.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Last-Planner-System-Workbook.pdf); https://projectproduction.org/glossary/percent-plan-complete-ppc/. PPC as weak progress indicator: lookahead-planning research summarised at https://www.ghafari.com/assets/documents/LCJ_16_013-Aligning-Near-Long-Term-Planning-for-LPS-Implementations.pdf. “Aim for 80% or more” taught as a practitioner guide figure, not a norm.
- Deming, Out of the Crisis (1986), p. 315: “94% belong to the system (responsibility of management), 6% special” — quoted at https://www.qimacros.com/lean-six-sigma-blog/deming-says-94-of-quality-depends-on-the-system/; the Deming Institute’s warning about quotes out of context: https://deming.org/the-transformation-is-everybodys-job/. Taught as his estimate from experience.
- Iceberg model: taught as “often attributed to Peter Senge; origin uncertain”.
- Gateway reviews (UK, OGC from 2001, now IPA): typically three days on site, sometimes four (https://www.finance-ni.gov.uk/articles/gateway-review-process); delivery confidence ratings in IPA reports, scale changed over the years (taught without naming the steps). “Eight to twelve interviews” and “three to five recommendations” are practitioner rules of thumb, hedged with “often” / “rule of thumb”.
- Cynefin: D. Snowden & M. Boone, “A Leader’s Framework for Decision Making”, HBR, November 2007 (https://store.hbr.org/product/leader-s-framework-for-decision-making/R0711C): simple (sense–categorise–respond), complicated (sense–analyse–respond), complex (probe–sense–respond), chaotic (act–sense–respond), disorder (break into parts). “Simple” later renamed “obvious”, then “clear”. Criticism (too vague to sort with) taught as a debate.
- Maurer: R. Maurer, Why Don’t You Want What I Want? (2002); three levels “I don’t get it / I don’t like it / I don’t like you” — summaries at https://www.mbadepot.com/why-resistance-matters/ and podcast notes; not checked against the book text.
- Prosci: active and visible sponsorship the top contributor in every Best Practices in Change Management report since 1998 — https://www.prosci.com/resources/articles/primary-sponsors-role-and-importance, https://www.prosci.com/change-management/thought-leadership-library/best-practices-in-change-management. Taught as surveys by a commercial firm, not experiments.
- Amabile & Kramer, The Progress Principle (HBR Press, 2011) and “The Power of Small Wins” (HBR, 2011): 238 people, 26 project teams, 7 companies, nearly 12,000 diary entries; progress in meaningful work the strongest driver; setbacks weigh more than progress; catalysts — https://lifestack.ai/blog/the-progress-principle-by-teresa-amabile-and-steven-kramer and other summaries. Limitation (creative knowledge work, less studied on the shop floor) stated in the lesson.
- MoSCoW and the ≤ 60% of effort for must haves — DSDM / Agile Business Consortium handbook ch. 10, via https://highberg.com/insights/the-moscow-method-for-prioritization and https://digitalcommunications.wp.st-andrews.ac.uk/2016/08/05/moscow-prioritisation-is-on-effort/; taught as “DSDM advises about 60%”.
- Pocket veto, meeting after the meeting, shuttle diplomacy, joint escalation, air cover: common practitioner terms, no single source; taught as practice (“sometimes called”).
