Index title: en 'Leading projects outside your field' / nl 'Projecten leiden buiten je vakgebied'. Spec: v13 plan, section 4.

Situations rotate over Merijn’s own work: an MES/ERP project at a metal parts maker, a medium-voltage connection with the grid operator (congestion, protection settings, switchgear lead time), and a palletising robot cell from an automation supplier (PLC, safety PLC, FAT/SAT). Every lesson has a `said` line and a pushback.

## Facts and sources
- Leary et al., “Cognitive and interpersonal features of intellectual humility”, *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* (2017).
- Kruger & Dunning, “Unskilled and unaware of it”, *JPSP* (1999); size of the effect debated (stated as such).
- Maister, Green & Galford, *The Trusted Advisor* (2000): trust = (credibility + reliability + intimacy) ÷ self-orientation; stated as a model, not a measurement.
- RACI: one Accountable per task (standard usage).
- Klein, “Performing a Project Premortem”, *HBR* (Sept 2007), citing Mitchell, Russo & Pennington (1989): prospective hindsight raised the ability to identify reasons by about 30%.
- Kahneman & Tversky (1979): planning fallacy. Reference class forecasting: Kahneman & Lovallo (1993), Flyvbjerg (2006).
- Flyvbjerg & Gardner, *How Big Things Get Done* (2023): database of ~16,000 projects; 8.5% on budget and on time; 0.5% on budget, time and benefits (checked against reviews in *The Independent Review* and *The Economist*).
- PERT three-point estimate (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6, US Navy Polaris programme, 1958; stated as a convention. Worked numbers: (6 + 32 + 16) ÷ 6 = 9; recall (4 + 24 + 14) ÷ 6 = 7.
- “Trust but verify” (doveryai, no proveryai): Russian proverb used by Reagan in the 1980s arms talks.
- Weinberg, *Quality Software Management: Systems Thinking* (1992): context-switching rule of thumb (2 projects ≈ 20% lost, 5 projects ≈ 75% lost); hedged as a rough guide.
- Pink, *Drive* (2009), building on Deci & Ryan’s self-determination theory: autonomy, mastery, purpose.
- Ninety-ninety rule: credited to Tom Cargill (Bell Labs), popularised by Jon Bentley, “Programming Pearls”, *Communications of the ACM*, Sept 1985.
- Scrum Guide (2020): Definition of Done. FAT/SAT: standard automation practice.
- Bezos, Amazon shareholder letters: 2015 (Type 1/Type 2, one-way/two-way doors), 2016 (“most decisions should probably be made with somewhere around 70% of the information you wish you had”).
- Pfeffer, *7 Rules of Power* (2022): get out of your own way, break the rules, show up in powerful fashion, create a powerful brand, network relentlessly, use your power, success excuses (almost) everything. Publisher wording “show up in powerful fashion” used. Mentioned in `decide` and `stakeholders` where he communicates a decision.
- Minto, *The Pyramid Principle* (McKinsey; book 1987).
- Watkins, *The First 90 Days* (2003). The 30-60-90 plan is stated as “a common way”, not as Watkins’s own format.
- MORE PIES: not attributed to an author (origin uncertain, as in pm.lead). Active listening: Rogers & Farson (1957), credited in pm.lead.
- Rules of thumb hedged: ask after hearing a word three times; a checkable result every two to four weeks; a premortem with no surprise means people haven’t been honest yet; 10% of time on content.
