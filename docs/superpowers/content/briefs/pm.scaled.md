# pm.scaled — Scaling: SAFe and beyond (projecten, track agile, level 3)

Index title: en 'Scaling: SAFe and beyond' / nl 'Opschalen: SAFe en verder'. Second module of the Projecten track `agile`, "Agile and IT projects" / "Agile en IT-projecten", placed after pm.agile (plan: docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-07-v13-listen-ict-outside-layout.md, section 3). Module `level: 3`: it builds on pm.agile (level 2), which introduces Scrum, backlogs and Kanban.

Format: topic module, 4 topics × 4 levels (Basis, Werkkennis, Gevorderd, Expert), as eng.machining and pm.agile. Worked examples at levels 1–2, faded at 3–4. Every lesson has a `said` line, a situation with pushback (pm style), 5 teach blocks ≤ 90 words, 1 pre question, 4 recall questions (one numeric per lesson), 4–6 terms in the same order in en and nl, each term defined once in the module. Situations are Merijn as a junior Lean consultant at manufacturers (machine builders, packaging, food, pumps, e-bikes, medical beds…).

## Facts and sources (checked 2026-11-07/08)

SAFe (scaledagileframework.com now redirects to framework.scaledagile.com; many full articles are behind a login, so only the public summaries could be read. The site points to “SAFe 6.0 Big Picture configurations”; nothing newer was found):
- ART: “a long-lived team of Agile teams” that “incrementally develops, delivers, and often operates one or more solutions in a development value stream”; “ARTs are generally made up of 50 -125 people”; key roles “Product Management, System Architect, Release Train Engineer (RTE), and Business Owners”. https://framework.scaledagile.com/agile-release-train/
- PI Planning: “a cadence-based event for the entire ART that aligns teams and stakeholders”, “typically a 2-day event”, PIs “every 8-12 weeks”, outputs committed PI objectives and “an ART planning board that reflects feature delivery dates and dependencies”, held in the Innovation and Planning Iteration. https://framework.scaledagile.com/pi-planning/
- RTE: “a servant leader and ART coach who facilitates ART events and processes”; facilitates PI Planning. https://framework.scaledagile.com/release-train-engineer/
- Product Management: defines “desirable, viable, feasible, and sustainable solutions”, manages and prioritises the ART backlog. https://framework.scaledagile.com/product-management/
- PI objectives: “either committed or uncommitted”. https://framework.scaledagile.com/pi-objectives/
- Essential SAFe: “the minimal elements necessary for Agile Release Trains to deliver solutions”, “the simplest starting point for implementation”. https://framework.scaledagile.com/essential-safe/
- 6.0 terminology table (Program Increment → Planning Interval, Program Board → ART Planning Board, Program Backlog → ART Backlog, Program Predictability Measure → ART Predictability Measure, Scrum of Scrums → Coach Sync, Daily Standup → Team Sync, APMO → Value Management Office); OKRs optional in the spanning palette. https://framework.scaledagile.com/whats-new-in-safe-6-0
- Epic: “a significant solution development initiative”; business vs enabler epics (“enhance the architectural runway”); each epic requires “an MVP, a Lean business case, and an estimate of costs”; managed in a portfolio Kanban, approved by portfolio leadership. https://framework.scaledagile.com/epic/ — the Portfolio Kanban states (Funnel, Reviewing, Analyzing, Ready, Implementing, Done) and the hypothesis-statement wording are from the SAFe 6.0 big picture and training material as commonly cited; the lesson says “roughly”/“ongeveer”.
- LPM: “aligns strategy and execution by applying Lean and systems thinking approaches to strategy and investment funding, Agile portfolio operations, and governance”. https://framework.scaledagile.com/lean-portfolio-management/
- Lean budgets: “a financial governance approach that funds value streams instead of projects”. https://framework.scaledagile.com/lean-budgets/ — the four guardrails (investment by horizon, capacity allocation, approval of significant initiatives, continuous Business Owner engagement) from SAFe’s guardrails article as quoted by secondary sources (agility-at-scale.com/safe/lpm/portfolio-guardrails/; cloudwards.net/lean-budget-guardrails). The threshold examples (half a million, two PIs) and the 60/25/15 split are illustrations, stated as “for example”.
- WSJF: “relative cost of delay divided by the relative job duration”, inputs “relative user and business value, time criticality, risk reduction and/or opportunity enablement, and job size”, ignores sunk costs, Reinertsen quote “If you only quantify one thing, quantify the Cost of Delay”. https://framework.scaledagile.com/wsjf/ — scale 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20 and “one column at a time, smallest item = 1” from the public v5 article (v5.scaledagileframework.com/wsjf); the lesson says “in the public SAFe article”.
- Business value 1–10 by Business Owners; ART predictability = actual / planned business value of committed objectives, 80–100% band; uncommitted objectives may add to actual value: consistent across secondary and training sources (open-exam-prep SAFe POPM guide, targetprocess.com guide, University of Tartu thesis on SAFe metrics); stated as “gangbare SAFe-richtlijn / common SAFe guidance”.
- Confidence vote “average ≥ 3” and ROAM expansion: training material; the threshold is stated as a rule of thumb (sources differ on handling individual 1s and 2s).
- SAFe first published 2011 (Dean Leffingwell); 6.0 released early 2023. State of Agile (Digital.ai) has listed SAFe as the most used scaling approach for years; stated with the caveat that it is a self-selected survey.

LeSS (https://less.works/less/framework/index, https://less.works/less/less-huge/index): “Up to eight teams (of eight people each)”; one Product Owner, one Product Backlog, “one Definition of Done for all teams”, “one Potentially Shippable Product Increment at the end of each Sprint”, common sprint; Sprint Planning One and Two; Overall Retrospective “maximum of 45 minutes per week of Sprint”; LeSS Huge for “more than eight teams” with Requirement Areas and Area PO. Creators Craig Larman and Bas Vodde (books 2008, 2010, “Large-Scale Scrum: More with LeSS” 2016); the site itself does not name them on these pages.

Nexus (https://www.scrum.org/resources/online-nexus-guide, January 2021): “approximately three to nine Scrum Teams that work together to deliver a single product”; single Product Owner and Product Backlog; Nexus Integration Team = PO, a Scrum Master and Nexus Integration Team Members, accountable for an Integrated Increment every sprint; events Cross-Team Refinement, Nexus Sprint Planning, Nexus Daily Scrum, Nexus Sprint Review, Nexus Sprint Retrospective; artefacts with commitments Product Backlog–Product Goal, Nexus Sprint Backlog–Nexus Sprint Goal, Integrated Increment–Definition of Done; “Ken Schwaber and Scrum.org developed Nexus”. That the Nexus Sprint Review replaces the individual team reviews is from the guide’s event section (not in the part fetched; well established).

Spotify: Kniberg & Ivarsson, “Scaling Agile @ Spotify with Tribes, Squads, Chapters & Guilds”, Oct 2012 (https://blog.crisp.se/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/SpotifyScaling.pdf, read in full): disclaimer “We didn’t invent this model… only a snapshot… a journey in progress, not a journey completed”; tribes “smaller than 100 people or so”, based on the Dunbar number; chapter lead is the line manager. Jeremiah Lee, “Spotify’s Failed #SquadGoals”, 19 April 2020 (https://www.jeremiahlee.com/posts/failed-squad-goals/): matrix management, chapter leads without delivery accountability, PMs without an engineering peer, autonomy without alignment, collaboration assumed; Joakim Sundén: “part ambition, part approximation” (attributed to Sundén, not Kniberg).

Criticism: Ken Schwaber, “unSAFe at any speed”, blog, August 2013, comparing SAFe to RUP (InfoQ https://www.infoq.com/news/2013/08/safe/ and agile42 summary; InfoQ notes the critics were not SAFe-trained — included for balance). Martin Fowler, “The State of Agile Software in 2018”, Agile Australia keynote, transcript 25 Aug 2018: “Agile Industrial Complex”, “imposing methods on people is an absolute travesty” (https://martinfowler.com/articles/agile-aus-2018.html). Putta, Paasivaara & Lassenius, “Benefits and Challenges of Adopting the Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe): Preliminary Results from a Multivocal Literature Review”, PROFES 2018 — mainly positive reported outcomes, many sources from SAFe’s own case-study collection (stated as “many”, not a number). Conway’s law: M. Conway, “How Do Committees Invent?”, Datamation, 1968.

Hybrid: R. G. Cooper, “Agile–Stage-Gate Hybrids”, Research-Technology Management 59(1), 2016, 21–29; R. G. Cooper & A. F. Sommer, “Agile–Stage-Gate for Manufacturers”, RTM 61(2), 2018, 17–26 (sprints, stand-ups, demos and retrospectives embedded in stages; early results positive in a handful of firms, with challenges). Stage-gate stages and go/kill/hold/recycle: Cooper, Winning at New Products. Water-Scrum-Fall: Dave West, Forrester, 2011. Set-based design: Ward, Sobek et al. research on Toyota (1990s), also a SAFe principle-level practice. A/B/C samples: common automotive-supplier practice, definitions vary per OEM (stated so). Mould lead times 8–20 weeks and the cost-of-change amounts are illustrations, stated as such, with “always ask the supplier”.

## Decisions

- Track: `TRACKS.pm` gets `'agile'` and i18n `track_agile` (en “Agile and IT projects”, nl “Agile en IT-projecten”) exactly as in the pm.agile branch, so the merge resolves to one entry. The module entry is appended after the existing pm modules; in the merged index it should sit after pm.agile.
- Module level 3, so recommended after pm.agile (level 2).
- SAFe terms are kept in English in the Dutch text too (Agile Release Train, PI Planning, Business Owners, Epic Owner…), as Dutch practitioners use them; Dutch words where the field uses Dutch (afhankelijkheid, cadans, waardestroom, capaciteitsallocatie, terugplannen, integratiepunt).
- 6.0 names are used throughout (Planning Interval, ART Planning Board, ART Backlog), with the old names mentioned once so Merijn recognises clients on older versions.
- Criticism is balanced: Schwaber and Fowler on one side, InfoQ’s note and the real benefits for large hardware organisations on the other; the evidence base is called thin rather than bad.
- Answer options were rebalanced: answer positions 15/14/17/18 over 64 multiple-choice questions; in nl the correct option is never the longest, in en in 8 of 64.
- Some terms (Epic, Stage-gate, Product Owner, Lean) are also defined in pm.agile; the once-per-module rule is per module.

Uncertain: exact current wording of SAFe articles behind the login (guardrails, WSJF scale, Portfolio Kanban states, predictability band) — taken from the public v5 article and consistent secondary sources and hedged in the text; the Nexus Sprint Review replacement sentence was not in the fetched part of the guide.
