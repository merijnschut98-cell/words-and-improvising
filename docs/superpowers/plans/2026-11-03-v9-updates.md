> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

## Review Focus

These are the five most likely ways v9 breaks. Each one has a test that pins it.

1. **The queue loses or corrupts questions.** Two risks: the long-gap change (Task 3) silently drops or resets existing `courseReviews`, and a v8 device syncing with a v9 device fights over box 5–6 entries. Pinned in Task 3: a v8 blob loads unchanged; a lapsed question returns instead of vanishing; `mergeStates` of a box-6 and a box-4 entry keeps the later `due`; owned still means box ≥ 4.
2. **Today pick regressions.** The new reasons `apply` (Task 1) and `prep` (Task 2) could starve lessons, loop daily, or count twice toward the streak. Pinned: a plan follow-up appears at most twice and then expires; prep runs once per event; a follow-up gives no XP and is not a round; a prep session counts once, like a due session.
3. **A second filled button sneaks onto a screen.** The candidates are the guessing toggle, the plan field, "New scene from my work", the pushback steps and the explain box. Pinned: the e2e helper `onePrimary(page)` runs on every new screen and state in this plan.
4. **Coach output or free text leaks or breaks rendering.** Risks: `settings.context` gets synced; a plan or pushback answer is sent without a tap; a generated scene with two rubric lines crashes the situation screen. Pinned in Tasks 1, 4 and 5: `syncable()` strips `context`; requests contain only the named fields; parsers reject malformed JSON and the flow falls back to the lesson's own content.
5. **Offline and no-key paths diverge from the key path.** For example, pushback without a key shows nothing, or a figure that 404s leaves an empty frame. Pinned: every coach feature has an e2e step **without** a key that runs the same screens from content; the figure load failure is caught (Task 7).

## Why these tasks (evidence)

Task order follows value to Merijn: getting what he learns into his week first, retention second, the coach and polish after.

**Transfer to his week (Tasks 1, 2)**
- *Implementation intentions.* If-then plans ("When the planning lead sends the data late again, I will name it on Monday") raise goal attainment with a medium-to-large average effect (d ≈ 0.65 over 94 tests). A 2025 update of 642 tests finds the effect larger when the plan is in if-then form and rehearsed. Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, summarised at https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905 and https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-research/.
- *Transfer of training.* A meta-analysis of 89 studies found transfer predicted by motivation and a supportive work environment. Predictors were stronger for open skills such as leadership than for closed ones, which is exactly the Projecten content. Blume, Ford, Baldwin & Huang 2010, J. of Management, https://www.scinapse.io/papers/2165041900.
- *Behaviour modelling training.* A meta-analysis of 117 studies found the largest transfer to job behaviour when trainees set specific on-the-job goals and when use of the skill was followed up. Taylor, Russ-Eft & Chan 2005, J. Applied Psychology, https://www.qic-wd.org/sites/default/files/Umbrella%20Summary%20-%20Behavior%20Modeling%20Training%20112421b.pdf.
- *Reflection.* Replacing 15 minutes of work with written reflection on the day raised performance by about 20 %, through task understanding and self-efficacy. Di Stefano, Gino, Pisano & Staats, "Making experience count", https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2414478.
- *Retrieval just before the moment.* Material learned by retrieval practice held up under acute stress, while re-read material did not. Smith, Floerke & Thomas 2016, Science, https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/11/161124160426.htm. Test-enhanced learning transfers to new formats and contexts (d ≈ 0.40), but the transfer disappears when initial accuracy is poor, so refresh what he has already learned rather than teach new material. Pan & Rickard 2018, Psych. Bulletin, https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zC1oBp6yE72b7YHzaJZmjXf.

**Long-term retention across three subjects (Task 3)**
- *Confidence and errors.* Errors made with high confidence are corrected more readily after feedback (the hypercorrection effect). A correct guess, though, is not retrieval: on a 4-option item it happens 25 % of the time by chance. Metcalfe 2017, Annual Review of Psychology, "Learning from errors", https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2023/8/3-1 and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercorrection_(psychology).
- *Gap length.* The optimal gap between reviews grows as the retention interval grows. For knowledge kept for a year, gaps of weeks to months beat a fixed 21 days. Cepeda, Vul, Rohrer, Wixted & Pashler 2008, Psych. Science, https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaVulRohrerWixtedPashler-PS-2008.pdf.
- *Successive relearning.* About three spaced relearning sessions to criterion give most of the benefit. Recalling once in each of three spaced sessions beat three recalls in one session by more than twice. This confirms the v8 "owned at box 4" rule; v9 keeps it and adds longer gaps after it. Rawson & Dunlosky 2022, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09637214221100484.
- *Interleaving.* The overall effect is moderate. It is larger when categories are similar to each other, and it is negative for words and expository text (g = −0.39). Brunmair & Richter 2019, Psych. Bulletin, https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf. **Decision:** keep v8's within-track interleaving and do **not** interleave across subjects. The evidence supports the spec as written.
- *Retrieval in practice.* 57 % of classroom effect sizes were medium or large, for multiple-choice and short-answer formats alike, with format and timing mattering little. Agarwal, Nunes & Blunt 2021, https://retrievalpractice.org/review. Practice tests beat restudy across 118 studies, with feedback adding to the effect. Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan 2017, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654316689306.

**Habit without new rewards (cuts across Tasks 1–3)**
- Tangible, expected rewards undermine intrinsic motivation (128 studies). Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999, https://home.ubalt.edu/ntygmitc/642/Articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf. No badges for plans or prep.
- Habits form over a median of 66 days (range 18–254), and missing a single day did not hurt. Lally et al. 2010, https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit. So: calm wording, no streak drama, and the daily step stays one tap from the start screen.

**Expert learning: cases, difficult conversations, reflection (Tasks 4, 6)**
- Role-play communication training with active practice of at least 6 hours: ES = 0.54 on behaviour across 13 trials. Barth & Lannen 2011, https://doc.rero.ch/record/300051/files/mdq441.pdf. Cochrane review, medical students: SMD 0.92 vs usual teaching. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33559127/.
- Case-based learning vs lectures: exam SMD ≈ 0.58–0.68 and critical thinking SMD 0.75, with high heterogeneity. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12492731 and https://www.ejgm.org/Effectiveness-of-case-based-learning-in-medical-and-pharmacy-education-A-meta-analysis,166519,0,2.html.
- Honest caveat: across professions, deliberate practice explained under 1 % of performance variance. Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald 2014, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4101876/. So pushback practice is short and specific (two turns, one rubric) and tied to his real week. It is not a grind of volume.

**The API key (Tasks 4, 5)**
- A structured AI tutor built on active-learning principles beat in-class active learning in a crossover RCT (194 students; more learning in less time). Kestin et al. 2025, Scientific Reports, https://scale.stanford.edu/publications/ai-tutoring-outperforms-active-learning.
- Unguarded GPT access raised practice scores but lowered exam scores by 17 % once removed. A tutor prompt that withheld answers and gave hints largely removed the harm. Bastani et al. 2025, PNAS, https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486. **Rule for every coach feature: he answers first; the coach never gives the model answer before his attempt.**
- Prompted self-explanation improves learning (64 studies, about 6,000 learners) and outperformed instructor explanation. Bisra et al. 2018, https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2018-bisra.pdf.

### Task 1: "Use it this week" — an if-then plan at the end of a lesson, and a check-back

**Value:** highest. This turns lessons into behaviour at Enexis and at manufacturing clients (Gollwitzer & Sheeran; Taylor et al.; Di Stefano et al.).

**Files:**
- Create: `src/ui/apply.js`, `tests/apply.test.js`
- Modify: `src/core/course.js` (plans, Today reason `apply`), `src/core/storage.js`, `src/core/sync.js`, `src/ui/lesson.js` (done card), `src/ui/coursehome.js` (`openPick`, `COURSE_ICON.apply`), `src/ui/progress.js` (one quiet line), `src/app.js` (screen `apply`), `src/i18n.js`, `styles.css`, `sw.js`, `e2e/smoke.mjs`
- Test: `tests/apply.test.js`, `tests/storage.test.js`, `tests/sync.test.js`, `tests/course.test.js`

**Interfaces:**
- State: `courses[subject].plans: Array<{ id, lessonId, text, day, due, asked: 0|1|2, outcome: null | { used: 'yes'|'partly'|'no', note, ts } }>`, capped at 50, newest first. `text` ≤ 160 chars and `note` ≤ 160 chars, both whitespace-collapsed.
- `addPlan(state, { subject, lessonId, text }, today, id) -> state`. Ignores empty text. `due` is the next working day at least 2 days after `today` (Mon–Fri; a plan made on Thursday is due Monday). One open plan per lesson: a new one replaces an open one for the same lesson.
- `answerPlan(state, { subject, id, used, note }, now) -> state`. Sets `outcome`; ignores unknown ids and invalid `used`.
- `skipPlan(state, { subject, id }, today) -> state`. `asked + 1` and `due = today + 2`; at `asked === 2` the plan expires (`outcome: { used: 'no', note: '', ts }`), so it never nags a third time.
- `openPlans(state, subject, today) -> plan[]`. Due, unanswered and not expired, oldest due first.
- `courseTodayPick` gains reason `apply` **first** in order, returning `{ reason: 'apply', planId, lessonId, moduleId }`. It is quick and time-sensitive, so it goes before `due ≥ 5`.
- `planStats(state, subject, since) -> { made, used }`. Progress shows it as one mono line ("Used at work: 3 of 5 plans, last 30 days"). There is no target and no badge.
- Sync: `plans` merge as a union by `id`. An entry with an `outcome` wins over one without; with both answered, the later `outcome.ts` wins. `asked` takes the maximum.
- XP and streak: making or answering a plan gives **0 XP and is not a round** (no new reward layer).
- UI, lesson done card: under the existing done content, one `textarea.plan-input` (2 rows, `maxlength` 160). Label "Where will you use this this week?" Placeholder in if-then form, taken from the lesson's `situation.scene` setting if present, else "When …, I will …". The plan saves when he taps the existing primary ("Back to course"). There is **no extra button**; an empty field saves nothing.
- UI, `apply` screen: the plan text (text node), three choice buttons (`.opt`: used / partly / not yet), an optional one-line note, then one recall question from that lesson through `renderQuiz` (retrieval right after the real experience). That question is scheduled like a due question but with `applyDue` disabled for XP. Use the new `applyRecall(state, …)`: schedule only, no XP, no round. One primary at a time: "Save" after the choice, then the quiz's own Next. A ghost "Ask me later" calls `skipPlan`.
- i18n (en+nl): `planLabel`, `planPh`, `applyTitle`, `applyUsed_yes|partly|no`, `applyNotePh`, `applyLater`, `applySave`, `courseWhy_apply`, `courseWhat_apply`, `planStats` (used, made) => …

### Task 3: Sure or guessing, longer gaps after owning, nothing lost after a break

**Value:** high for retention across three subjects. A correct guess no longer counts as owning (Metcalfe 2017). Owned knowledge comes back at growing gaps (Cepeda 2008). A long holiday no longer silently drops questions.

**Finding from the code (v8 behaviour):** `dueCourseQuestions` filters out anything due more than 60 days ago, and `maintenanceQuestion` only looks at owned questions. So after a two-month break every unowned question disappears from the queue for good. This task fixes that.

### Task 4: "They push back" — two turns of holding your ground after a situation

**Value:** high for his assertiveness goal and the team-captain role. It is short, specific deliberate practice with feedback (Barth & Lannen 2011; Cochrane 2021; Taylor 2005), sized modestly because of Macnamara 2014.

**Files:**
- Create: `src/core/pushback.js`, `tests/pushback.test.js`
- Modify: `src/content/courses/validate.js` (optional `situation.pushback`), the nine `src/content/courses/pm/*.{en,nl}.js` files (add `pushback` to every lesson's situation), `src/ui/situation.js` (pushback steps after the model answer), `src/i18n.js`, `styles.css`, `sw.js`, `e2e/smoke.mjs`
- Test: `tests/coursecontent.test.js`, `tests/pushback.test.js`

**Interfaces:**
- Content: `situation.pushback?: { lines: [string, string], model: string, rubric: [string, string, string] }`. The two lines are objections in the other person's voice, each ≤ 40 words. `model` is a calm, firm reply ≤ 70 words. The rubric is fixed per lesson, in the shape "Acknowledges the point", "Restates the ask or position clearly", "Proposes a concrete next step". The validator checks shapes, lengths and no `<`/`>` only when `pushback` is present; it is required for every `pm.*` lesson and optional for `eng.*`.
- `src/core/pushback.js`:
  - `pushbackTurns(situation) -> string[]` returns the offline lines;
  - `buildPushbackRequest({ lesson, turns, lang, model, context })` sends the scene, the ask, his answers so far and (only if set and he tapped) `settings.context`; the system prompt plays the other person, 1–2 sentences, realistic, never coaching, and never writes his reply for him (Bastani guardrail); JSON `{ reply, end }`;
  - `parsePushback`;
  - `buildPushbackGrade` reuses the `buildGradeRequest` shape with the pushback rubric, returning `{ points[3], missed }`;
  - `runPushback` and `runPushbackGrade`, both never throwing.
- UI flow on the situation screen, after the model answer: a ghost "They push back" (secondary, so the screen's primary stays "Done"). On tap the screen enters pushback mode, where the primary becomes "Reply":
  1. Show objection 1 (coach-generated with a key; content line 1 without one, or on error).
  2. He replies (textarea or mic, reusing `speech.js`).
  3. Show objection 2, and he replies again.
  4. Grade: coach grade with a key, otherwise self-rating checkboxes on the pushback rubric.
  5. Show the pushback `model`.
  6. "Done".
  Two turns maximum. Saves `lessons[id].pushback = { pts, ts }`, with 0 XP and no round. Sync merges it like `situation` (later ts wins).
