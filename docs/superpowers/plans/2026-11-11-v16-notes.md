## Film rules

## Module intro films (v17)
- **Purpose.** Merijn, 13 Nov: "For most lessons (and specifically subjects) it's important that there is an introduction video. The example of machining is a good one. That one is most like an introduction to machining, to later dive into various subjects of machining. You're now sometimes skipping that introduction, diving straight into deeper material and calculations, without the high-over picture. That high-over picture is the thing I need the most." A module intro film is that high-over picture, shown at the top of the opened module, above its topics (`filmStep`: video, else HTML film, else the blurb).
- **The model: eng.machining.overview** ("Van staaf tot as"). One gearbox shaft follows its routing across the floor (saw, lathe, machining centre, hardening, grinder); every later deep dive has a place on that map. Copy the approach, not the drawing.
- **Story pattern.** ONE concrete project or thing travels through the whole module, in the module's own order, and the film shows at each step which topic covers it and why it matters. The map before the detail, few numbers, no calculations, no lesson's worked example (an intro that becomes a deep dive in disguise is the anti-goal). Use the Basis lessons' definitions, worded as the lessons word them. Make it realistic for Merijn's manufacturing world. Pilot: pm.plan.intro, "Lijn 3 verhuist": a packing line moves to a new hall in the three-week summer shutdown (goal; WBS with deliverables, work packages and what is out of scope; dependencies, the outside power supply as critical path, a go/no-go milestone; a range, effort against duration and one visible buffer; a risk register with a switchboard that may be late, one owner, a mitigation; the four pictures as one plan), ending on the map.
- **Length (Merijn, 14 Nov).** A film is as long as its content needs; a minute or even half a minute is fine when that is enough. There is no length to fill. An intro has to carry: each topic in a scene of its own (at least two beats each, the kit checks it), how the topics hang together, the map and where to start. Say each thing once: the pilot's review cut a question asked twice, an answer given twice and a fourth listing of the topics (259 s to 247 s, 621 to 592 nl and 646 to 615 en words, 7 scenes). Trim repetition, not content. Scenes: one per topic, an opening scene (the goal and the route), a "one plan" scene and a closing map scene. The kit's floors are now 25 s, 2 to 9 scenes and 25 s of narration (about 60 words, films/_kit/assert.mjs; Task 4a), so a very short intro builds: give the brief no length to fill, say what the film has to carry and let the length follow. Do not change the limits in a film build. The closing scene is the shared map: the kit's `mapScene({ subject, highlight })` (films/_kit/mapscene.mjs, the app's own `mapSvg`, both languages in one drawing, the highlighted places lit as cue groups `map-<slug>`) plus `mapStyles()` for the page's CSS (the film page does not load styles.css). Field films end on it too. Keep the scene in ONE function of the stage script (pilot: `closing` in films/pm.plan.intro/stage.js): the map, zoomed to the lit place, then the module's topics with the four levels as columns, labelled with the app's own level words (`levelWords(lang)` from mapscene.mjs, which reads src/i18n.js: Basis, Werkkennis, Gevorderd, Expert; Basics, Working knowledge, Advanced, Expert). ## Film length (Merijn, 14 Nov 2026)

"Videos do not always need to be long. If a video of a minute or even half a minute is enough, that is sufficient."

- A film is as long as its content needs. Do not pad to reach a length and do not stretch a thin topic to fill 3 minutes. Say in the brief what the film has to carry and let the length follow.
- Rough guide, not a target: a module intro about 30 to 90 seconds when the module is small, up to 3 minutes when it carries many parts; a field film 2 to 3 minutes; a deep-dive lesson film as long as its teach text and worked example need.
- The 22-unit stage text, the 4 s per beat and the guard limits stay. Short films are therefore short in beats, not in readability.
- The kit builds short films (Task 4a, 14 Nov). `films/_kit/assert.mjs`: `MIN_TOTAL` 25 s (was 60), 2 to 9 scenes (`MIN_SCENES`, was 4), `NARRATION_MIN` 25 s of narration, about 60 words (was 60 s, about 150 words). `MAX_TOTAL` 300, `NARRATION_MAX` 330 and the guard limits (pause in a sentence at most 0.4 s, WER at most 15 percent, at most 360 s per language) are unchanged, and so is every per-beat, readability and caption check. A film of 1 scene still fails. The module intro rule (each topic two beats in its own scene) and the field rule (each station) stay, so a short intro is short in topics, not in checks. The rendered video follows the narration audio, not the designed scene lengths, and the Dutch voice speaks about 3 words a second against the kit's estimate of 2.5: a throwaway 30 s, 72-word review film rendered 24.9 s in Dutch and 30.8 s in English (listen guard PASS, WER 1.4 percent both), and a film at the narration floor (about 62 words) renders about 21 s in Dutch.
