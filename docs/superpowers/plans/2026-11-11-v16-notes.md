## Film rules
- 2026-11-12 (Merijn: "het relevante onderdeel highlighten, wit / lichtblauw, dan weet de leerling waar hij moet kijken"): every sentence gets a focus highlight. Each beat names the part(s) it explains with `focus: 'key'` or `focus: ['a', 'b']` (keys of the stage's `data-cue` groups), or `focus: null` for an overview sentence; a term's cue joins the beat's focus. While the beat is spoken and its caption shows, the player lights those parts white / light blue with a soft halo (`--fp-focus`, `--fp-focus-glow`, `--fp-focus-top`, `--fp-focus-shade`, `--fp-focus-text`, light and dark values), eased in over 0.4 s, held still with reduced motion and in render mode. Documented in films/_kit/build-film.mjs.
- New films use `strictFocus: true`: the build then fails on a beat without a focus field, on a legacy `cues` list, on a term in an overview beat, or on a key that is no part of the drawing. Older films print warning counts instead (beats that light nothing; cue keys the drawing does not have). Reference film: eng.switchgear.protection.
- 2026-11-12, legibility measured (the v14 rule "labels on the stage at least 12 px at 390 px", docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-07-v14-explainer-films.md): the 12 px is the rendered height of the text's font size in the lesson view, measured in the app at a 390×844 viewport, deviceScaleFactor 1, as font-size units × the stage SVG's on-screen scale (stage width / 640), not on the film page alone. ## Module intro films (v17)
- **Purpose.** Merijn, 13 Nov: "For most lessons (and specifically subjects) it's important that there is an introduction video. The example of machining is a good one. That one is most like an introduction to machining, to later dive into various subjects of machining. You're now sometimes skipping that introduction, diving straight into deeper material and calculations, without the high-over picture. That high-over picture is the thing I need the most." A module intro film is that high-over picture, shown at the top of the opened module, above its topics (`filmStep`: video, else HTML film, else the blurb).
- **The model: eng.machining.overview** ("Van staaf tot as"). One gearbox shaft follows its routing across the floor (saw, lathe, machining centre, hardening, grinder); every later deep dive has a place on that map. Copy the approach, not the drawing.
- **Story pattern.** ONE concrete project or thing travels through the whole module, in the module's own order, and the film shows at each step which topic covers it and why it matters. The map before the detail, few numbers, no calculations, no lesson's worked example (an intro that becomes a deep dive in disguise is the anti-goal). Use the Basis lessons' definitions, worded as the lessons word them. Make it realistic for Merijn's manufacturing world. Pilot: pm.plan.intro, "Lijn 3 verhuist": a packing line moves to a new hall in the three-week summer shutdown (goal; WBS with deliverables, work packages and what is out of scope; dependencies, the outside power supply as critical path, a go/no-go milestone; a range, effort against duration and one visible buffer; a risk register with a switchboard that may be late, one owner, a mitigation; the four pictures as one plan), ending on the map.
- **Targets.** About 3 to 4 minutes (pilot: 240 s), roughly 450 to 600 narration words per language (pilot: 572 nl, 595 en), 5 to 8 scenes (pilot: 7). One scene per topic, an opening scene (the goal and the route), a "one plan" scene and a closing map scene. Keep the closing scene in ONE function of the stage script (pilot: `mapScene` in films/pm.plan.intro/stage.js); v17 task 3 replaces it with the shared map scene.
- **Film rules still apply.** 16:9, simple figures (the shared pm world and films/pm.agile.scrum/cast.mjs, imported not copied), stage text at least 22 units, `strictFocus: true` (every beat a focus, every term a cue), every card labelled voorbeeld or schema, white and light-blue focus, page under 300,000 bytes, no reserved fp-* classes, nothing spoken in parentheses or symbols, few colons and comma lists in the narration (the render guard caps a pause inside a sentence at 0.4 s; the pilot's build checks it), nl and en matching.
- **Defining one with the kit.** In `build.mjs`, `defineFilm({ id: '<module id>.intro', module: '<module id>', topics: { <topic id>: { scene: 's2', cue: 'tBreak' }, ... }, scenes, strictFocus: true })` instead of `lessons`. `topics` has one entry per topic of the module: the scene that explains it and the `data-cue` group (a tile that carries its title) that one of that scene's beats lights. The film then serves all of the module's lessons, its `{t:Term}` come from any of them, and its `{n:}` numbers from any lesson text or from the module's own title, blurb and topic titles (`moduleTextOf`, films/_kit/assert.mjs). Page data carries `module` and `topics` (with the index titles); lesson films' data is unchanged (every existing page rebuilds byte-identical). Tests: tests/introfilm.test.js (the kit on a made-up module), tests/filmcontent.test.js ("every shipped intro film", rechecked against the current module text, as lesson films are).
- **Coverage rule (replaces per-term coverage).** Every topic of the module is on the film's map, still under its index title, named in a caption or on a card (the page's stage text) in both languages, and shown: a beat of its scene lights its part. A renamed or added topic, or a vanished number, fails the content test until the film is rebuilt.

## Film length (Merijn, 14 Nov 2026)

"Videos do not always need to be long. If a video of a minute or even half a minute is enough, that is sufficient."

- A film is as long as its content needs. Do not pad to reach a length and do not stretch a thin topic to fill 3 minutes. Say in the brief what the film has to carry and let the length follow.
- Rough guide, not a target: a module intro about 30 to 90 seconds when the module is small, up to 3 minutes when it carries many parts; a field film 2 to 3 minutes; a deep-dive lesson film as long as its teach text and worked example need.
- The 22-unit stage text, the 4 s per beat and the guard limits stay. Short films are therefore short in beats, not in readability.
- The kit currently refuses short films (`MIN_TOTAL` 60 s, 4 to 9 scenes, `NARRATION_MIN` 60 words in `films/_kit/assert.mjs`). Task 4a of the v17 plan lowers these; until then the existing limits apply.
